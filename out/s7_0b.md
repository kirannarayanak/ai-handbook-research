**06 Legal Affairs** (Specialist; high confidence)

    - Lease and contract summaries: extraction templates run across a contract folder, then attorney review, to find critical terms and deadlines [1]
    - Litigation support: locating operative clauses, building chronologies from correspondence, spotting notice defects, turning a day-long first pass into a focused review [1]
    - Deposition transcript analysis with page-line citations and inconsistency flags, treated as draft work product [1]
    - Tabular reviews of contracts and production sets as a more advanced stage once workflows are tested (not individually verified) [1]
    - Starting with lower-risk repetitive tasks (summaries, first-pass review) under an AI-use policy with mandatory attorney review [1]
- **Capstone deliverable:** A clause-comparison and contract-summary pack for five sample contracts against a playbook: extraction table, deviations list, suggested redlines, with every citation checked against the source, a hallucination log, and an AI-use policy page covering privilege and confidentiality. Include prompts and time saved against manual review. Specialist-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Attorney-client privilege and work product, confidential client and company information, personal data (GDPR) and trade secrets. In an ACC global survey of in-house counsel, 56% believed AI tools could compromise privilege and generative AI was seen as the highest-risk category (49%) [2]. Rules: approved enterprise tools with contractual limits on data use, retention and training; review vendor terms (ABA Formal Opinion 512, 2024) [1]; never rely on AI legal research without verification, because of hallucinated citations [1]; obtain consent before putting client data into insufficiently protected tools [1].
- **Adoption KPIs:** Contract review turnaround time; share of AI outputs with all citations verified; number of privilege or confidentiality incidents (target zero); share of matters using the approved workflow.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Prioritise Thomson Reuters CoCounsel (legal research with citations; not verified by an opened source), Microsoft Word Copilot (redlines in the document where lawyers work), Claude (long-contract analysis) and NotebookLM (source-grounded Q&A). Most Specialist hours go to verification, privilege rules and policy design.
- **Tool-list fit:** Good fit. Word Copilot is narrow but sensible for drafting. Gemini is generic and its enterprise terms need checking. The handbook omits dedicated contract-AI and legal AI platforms such as Harvey, described in a vendor source for diligence (see department 03 source); no opened legal-department source ranks tools, so treat as not verified.

**09 Ethics & Investigations** (Advanced; medium confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Whistleblower response: interpreting complaints, generating follow-up questions and identifying relevant documents [1]
    - Early-stage triage: identifying and summarising key documents to decide whether a full investigation is needed (not individually verified) [1]
    - Time-sensitive matters: chronologies, patterns and hot documents to prioritise review (not individually verified) [1]
    - Detecting anomalous content such as code words or unusual calendar entries in conduct and fraud cases [1]
    - AI-powered translation inside case management so non-English reports can be investigated without waiting (vendor claim) [2]
    - Teams are cautiously adopting AI in intake, case management and reporting, per a 2024 benchmarking report [3]
- **Capstone deliverable:** An investigation case file for a synthetic complaint: neutral case summary, evidence timeline, policy-comparison table and list of follow-up interview questions, all with source references. Include a note on what AI was and was not permitted to see (anonymisation step), prompts, human reviewer corrections and time saved. Advanced-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Whistleblower identities, allegations about named individuals, health and employment data, privileged investigation material. Regulatory hooks: employment law, GDPR, whistleblower protection (the EU Whistleblower Directive and updated US Department of Justice guidance are noted in [2]) and legal privilege. Rules: minimise and pseudonymise data before AI use; AI must not make findings of fact or disciplinary recommendations; human oversight is stressed [1]; model type, jurisdiction and risk profile all affect whether AI is appropriate [1].
- **Adoption KPIs:** Days to complete case triage; time to build an evidence timeline; share of cases where AI use was documented and reviewed; reviewer correction rate; zero confidentiality breaches.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Prioritise Claude and Microsoft 365 Copilot (summaries and timelines inside a secure tenant), ServiceNow Now Assist if cases live in ServiceNow, and a short module on ChatGPT Enterprise only where approved. Spend most time on confidentiality, anonymisation and human decision rules.
- **Tool-list fit:** General assistants fit drafting tasks but are a weaker fit for evidence handling than purpose-built investigation or case-management platforms. The handbook omits those (for example Case IQ and Mitratech, the vendors in [2][3]; vendor sources). ChatGPT Enterprise and Gemini need privilege and data-residency review before use on case files.

**10 ESG & Sustainability** (Advanced; medium confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - CSRD gap analysis: Ekimetrics' ESG Copilot consolidated data from 20 reports against 1,200+ disclosure requirements, taking 6 weeks instead of about 6 months (vendor case) [1]
    - Consolidating scattered sustainability data into the CSRD reporting matrix and scoring existing reporting (scoring not individually verified) [1]
    - Recommendations for remediating data points where reporting does not meet requirements [1]
    - Drafting ESG report sections and narratives from validated data, with assurance evidence captured at the point of AI output (narrative drafting not individually verified) [2]
    - Emissions and metric calculations in AI workflows, which must remain traceable under third-party assurance [2]
- **Capstone deliverable:** A disclosure-gap and narrative pack for three ESRS data points: gap table against supplied source reports, AI-drafted narrative with every figure linked to source, and an evidence log recording model version, inputs, validation and approver. Include prompts and time saved. Advanced-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Emissions and supplier data, unpublished targets and performance (MNPI for listed firms), and personal data in social metrics (GDPR). CSRD requires third-party assurance of disclosures, so AI workflows need lineage, version history and control evidence [2]. Rules: record model version, source inputs, validation and approval metadata when the metric is created; apply change control to prompts, calculation rules and data-source substitutions [2]; restrict edit and publish rights; avoid generic narrative that does not reflect the firm's actual position. Note: [2] is a community post citing a vendor (Openlayer), so treat as secondary; the current status of CSRD scope and timing should be checked against primary EU sources (not verified).
- **Adoption KPIs:** Weeks to complete a disclosure gap analysis; share of reported data points with full lineage; assurance findings related to AI-produced figures; hours spent drafting narrative sections.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Prioritise Claude and Gemini (long-document extraction and gap analysis), Power BI Copilot (emissions data narratives) and Microsoft 365 Copilot. Give a dedicated block to assurance and audit trail, since this is the main constraint [2].
- **Tool-list fit:** Mostly fits. Gamma is a weak fit for the compliance-critical core and only helps with presentation. The handbook omits dedicated ESG data and reporting software; the only example opened is a consultancy's tool (ESG Copilot) [1], so any broader claim is not verified. ChatGPT Enterprise is fine as a general drafter.
