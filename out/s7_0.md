## Department cluster deep dives

All 60 departments, grouped into eight functional clusters. For each department: real-world AI use cases with sources, a capstone that fits the handbook's assignment template, the data rules the function must follow, adoption indicators, where the training hours should go, and whether the handbook's tool list fits.

### Leadership, strategy and governance

All ten departments work mostly with long, unstructured, high-stakes documents (board papers, contracts, filings, case files, deal data rooms), so the shared AI pattern is summarise, compare, extract and draft, with a named human accountable for the output. The sources agree on the same controls: use approved enterprise tools, treat outputs as claims to verify (COSO's framing), keep an inventory of use cases, and scale human review to risk. Shared risks are privilege waiver and discoverability of AI transcripts, material non-public information and deal confidentiality, personal data in investigations (GDPR, whistleblower protections), and AI output that cannot be traced for audit or assurance. Evidence is thinnest for Office of the CEO, ERM and Ethics, where findings come from practitioner or vendor pieces rather than studies. Train together on a common module covering data classification, verification and prompt/output logging, with joint labs for Board Secretariat, Legal and Compliance (records and privilege), for Compliance, Internal Audit and ERM (controls evidence and COSO), and for Corporate Strategy and Corporate Development (scenario and deal analysis).

*Fact-check:* I opened every cited URL (all returned OPENED) and checked the most surprising claims against the page text: EY adoption, Deloitte percentages, COSO six steps, Nestle and BCG figures, ACC survey, CSC survey, Ekimetrics figures, Optro 49%, Nasdaq Boardvantage, Harvey data-room claims. Nearly all checked claims held; corrections were small (wording, one unsupported use case, one unsupported sample size). Tool-capability claims (Datasite AI, CoCounsel, Acrobat AI, Now Assist) have no opened source and are flagged as not verified. Several sources are vendor or commentator pieces, so confidence is capped at medium for Compliance, Ethics and ESG. 11 correction(s) or removal(s) were applied.

**01 Office of the CEO** (Core; medium confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Executive assistant use of Microsoft 365 Copilot to summarise meetings and materials, plan the week and prepare client meetings; at EY Oceania this made leadership meetings more efficient and nearly 70% of 9,000 staff adopted the tool [1]
    - Real-time meeting summaries with agreements, disagreements and suggested actions, so executives can leave a meeting and catch up on return [1]
    - Drafting and summarising communications and reports, and analysing data in Outlook, Teams, Word and Excel, taught through role-specific 1:1 training at a global manufacturer (the specific apps and tasks are not verified on the opened page) [2]
    - Drafting a first cut of a difficult conversation or message, which the executive then rewrites in their own voice [1]
    - Selecting a small number of high-impact use cases tied to business goals, with a roadmap and KPIs, as a CEO-CIO joint task (2023 commentary, background only; page opened, specifics not individually checked) [3]
- **Capstone deliverable:** A weekly CEO briefing pack for one real (or anonymised) week: a one-page decision brief, a KPI narrative with every figure traced to its source, and a meeting-decision log. Submit the documented prompts, the verification checklist showing each number checked against source, and a measured before/after time log (target: at least 40% reduction against a baseline week). Sized for Core-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Board and strategy papers, unannounced results and deals (material non-public information under securities rules), personal data of executives and staff (GDPR), and privileged legal advice. Rules: use only the licensed enterprise assistant inside the tenant; never paste MNPI into public tools; recorded meetings need consent and a retention rule; the executive office owns verification of every number before it reaches the CEO.
- **Adoption KPIs:** Share of executive-office staff using the approved assistant weekly; hours saved per weekly briefing against baseline; share of briefs with all figures source-checked; number of decision-log entries completed within 24 hours of the meeting.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Spend most hours on hands-on drafting and summarising in Microsoft 365 Copilot (the tool leaders actually have in Outlook and Teams), then Claude or Gemini for long-document synthesis, with one short block on verifying numbers. Power BI Copilot only if the office owns KPI dashboards. Concurrency's case shows short, role-specific 1:1 sessions helped executive adoption [2].
- **Tool-list fit:** The list fits well; Microsoft 365 Copilot is the strongest fit given the cited executive deployments [1][2]. Gamma and ChatGPT Enterprise are optional extras for this function. No opened source names a widely used omitted tool, so omissions are not verified. Source [3] is from 2023, so use it for background only.

**02 Corporate Strategy** (Advanced; high confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Generating multiple scenarios quickly from financial data, news, filings and customer sentiment, instead of weeks of spreadsheet modelling [1]
    - Dynamic, updatable scenario models, including unconventional outcomes that human planners filter out [1]
    - Market and competitive analysis from news, financial reports and industry data, such as generative-search SWOT prompts [2]
    - Strategic risk simulation of downturns, supply-chain disruption and competitive threats to build contingency plans (supply-chain and downturn specifics not individually verified) [2]
    - Idea validation: Nestle's proprietary genAI tool cut product ideation from six months to six weeks (as reported by AlphaSense) [2]
- **Capstone deliverable:** A strategy memo with a three-scenario analysis for one real or case-study market question. Include a source table where every external claim is checked against an original document, the prompt log, and a short note on where the AI view was challenged or corrected, plus time saved against a manual estimate. Advanced-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Unreleased strategy, M&A intentions and competitor analysis that may be material non-public information; third-party licensed research whose terms may restrict AI use; and possible antitrust issues if competitor data is gathered improperly. Rules: no MNPI in non-enterprise tools; cite only opened primary sources; label model-generated assumptions as such.
- **Adoption KPIs:** Scenarios produced per planning cycle; days from question to first draft memo; share of memo claims with verified citations; number of AI-surfaced scenarios adopted into plans.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Prioritise Perplexity Enterprise (sourced research), Claude (long-document synthesis and structured scenario writing), Power BI Copilot (data-backed scenarios) and Gamma only for final board decks. Put the most hours on verification: a BCG experiment cited by AlphaSense found a 23% performance drop on business problem-solving, mainly because participants trusted misleading outputs [2].
- **Tool-list fit:** The list fits. Having both Perplexity and Perplexity Enterprise is redundant for corporate use. AlphaSense is cited as a strategy-specific research platform that the handbook omits [2], though it is a vendor source. Gamma is a weak fit for analysis (it is presentation only).

**03 Corporate Development & M&A** (Specialist; high confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Reviewing every document in a data room, extracting key provisions and flagging non-standard terms into structured reports within hours rather than weeks (vendor claim) [2]

- **Capstone deliverable:** A due-diligence summary for a sample (public or synthetic) data room of at least 20 contracts: an extraction table of key terms, a red-flag list with page-level citations, an assumptions log for valuation inputs, and a first integration tracker. Submit prompts, a verification sample (at least 20% of extracted items checked by hand with error rate reported) and time saved. Specialist-tier hours.

The handbook omits legal-diligence platforms such as Harvey, which its own vendor page describes for data-room review [2] (vendor source, treat claims with care). Gemini and ChatGPT Enterprise are generic and only suitable if deal-data terms are cleared.

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Summarising board materials to highlight key information; Nasdaq added Azure OpenAI summarisation to Boardvantage in 2024 [3]
    - Distilling lengthy board papers into clear summaries and supporting agenda planning [1]
    - Drafting board minutes from meetings, with human cross-checking before circulation [1]
    - Administration: summarising meetings, transcripts, notes and board packs, plus regulatory scanning and alerts [2]
    - Drafting routine narrative sections of the annual report, with a warning that generic AI text may not reflect company-specific risks [1]
- **Capstone deliverable:** A mock board pack and minutes set: AI-drafted summary of three board papers, draft minutes from a supplied transcript, and an action register, plus a one-page AI-use policy stating what counts as the official minute, where AI drafts are stored and when they are deleted. Include prompts, a line-by-line accuracy check against the transcript, and time saved. Core-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Board deliberations, MNPI, privileged legal advice and director personal data (GDPR). AI transcripts and summaries raise questions about which document is the official record, retention and later disclosure in litigation; the claim that sharing AI output beyond the core group risks waiving privilege is not verified from an opened page. Governance bodies advise that AI use sit within policies on accountability and content ownership [4]. Rules: define the formal minutes, restrict who receives AI output, and exclude privileged sessions from AI recording.
- **Adoption KPIs:** Hours to produce a board pack; days from meeting to approved minutes; number of corrections found in AI-drafted minutes; share of secretariat staff trained and following the AI-use policy.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Prioritise Microsoft Teams Copilot and Microsoft 365 Copilot (meeting capture and drafting), Adobe Acrobat AI Assistant (searching PDF board packs; not verified by an opened source) and Claude for long-paper summaries. Most time should go to record-keeping rules and verification, because the risk lies in minutes and privilege, not drafting speed.
- **Tool-list fit:** The list fits, but omits dedicated board-portal AI (for example Nasdaq Boardvantage) [3], which is where many boards already keep their papers. NotebookLM is useful for document search but its enterprise data terms should be checked before board material goes in (not verified). Gemini and Claude are general tools and need secure-tenant approval.

**05 Enterprise Risk Management** (Advanced; high confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Mapping GenAI risks to the existing control framework: COSO's 2026 guidance organises uses by capability, such as data extraction and ingestion and insight generation, with owners assigned per capability [1][2]
    - Maintaining an inventory of AI use cases with ownership and escalation, including detecting shadow AI [1]
    - Continuous monitoring of AI performance through indicators such as transaction volume, size and override percentages to detect model drift [1]
    - Treating AI output as a claim to validate rather than a fact, with risk assessment and change control moving toward continuous [2]
- **Capstone deliverable:** An AI-assisted risk register extract: 15 risks for one business unit drafted from supplied policies and incident notes, each with cause, impact, control mapping and owner, plus a trend summary. Include prompts, a reviewer sign-off on each risk, a comparison of AI versus manual drafting time, and a mapping of the team's own AI use cases to COSO's six steps (govern, inventory, assess, design, implement, monitor). Advanced-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Incident records, loss data, third-party and cyber vulnerability information, and risk appetite, which may include MNPI and personal data (GDPR). Rules: risk entries remain owned by named humans; no sensitive vulnerability detail in non-enterprise tools; log prompts and approvals so the AI-assisted register can itself be audited (SOX relevance where it supports internal control over financial reporting is general practice, not verified from an opened source).
- **Adoption KPIs:** Time to refresh the risk register per cycle; share of risks with mapped controls and owners; number of AI use cases in the inventory with a risk rating; override or correction rate on AI-drafted entries.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Prioritise Claude and Microsoft 365 Copilot (drafting and classifying risk text from documents), ServiceNow Now Assist if the firm's risk register lives in ServiceNow, and Power BI Copilot for trend narratives. Give extra time to the COSO six-step roadmap [1][2], since ERM will own AI risk governance.
- **Tool-list fit:** Broadly fits. ServiceNow Now Assist is only a good fit where ServiceNow GRC is the system of record (no opened source confirms this). Gemini is generic. No opened source names a widely used omitted ERM tool; not verified.

**06 Legal Affairs** (Specialist; high confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Lease and contract summaries: extraction templates run across a contract folder, then attorney review, to find critical terms and deadlines [1]
    - Litigation support: locating operative clauses, building chronologies from correspondence, spotting notice defects, turning a day-long first pass into a focused review [1]
    - Deposition transcript analysis with page-line citations and inconsistency flags, treated as draft work product [1]
    - Tabular reviews of contracts and production sets as a more advanced stage once workflows are tested (not individually verified) [1]
    - Starting with lower-risk repetitive tasks (summaries, first-pass review) under an AI-use policy with mandatory attorney review [1]
- **Capstone deliverable:** A clause-comparison and contract-summary pack for five sample contracts against a playbook: extraction table, deviations list, suggested redlines, with every citation checked against the source, a hallucination log, and an AI-use policy page covering privilege and confidentiality. Include prompts and time saved against manual review. Specialist-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Attorney-client privilege and work product, confidential client and company information, personal data (GDPR) and trade secrets. In an ACC global survey of in-house counsel, 56% believed AI tools could compromise privilege and generative AI was seen as the highest-risk category (49%) [2]. Rules: approved enterprise tools with contractual limits on data use, retention and training; review vendor terms (ABA Formal Opinion 512, 2024) [1]; never rely on AI legal research without verification, because of hallucinated citations [1]; obtain consent before putting client data into insufficiently protected tools [1].
- **Adoption KPIs:** Contract review turnaround time; share of AI outputs with all citations verified; number of privilege or confidentiality incidents (target zero); share of matters using the approved workflow.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Prioritise Thomson Reuters CoCounsel (legal research with citations; not verified by an opened source), Microsoft Word Copilot (redlines in the document where lawyers work), Claude (long-contract analysis) and NotebookLM (source-grounded Q&A). Most Specialist hours go to verification, privilege rules and policy design.
- **Tool-list fit:** Good fit. Word Copilot is narrow but sensible for drafting. Gemini is generic and its enterprise terms need checking. The handbook omits dedicated contract-AI and legal AI platforms such as Harvey, described in a vendor source for diligence (see department 03 source); no opened legal-department source ranks tools, so treat as not verified.

    - Whistleblower response: interpreting complaints, generating follow-up questions and identifying relevant documents [1]
    - Early-stage triage: identifying and summarising key documents to decide whether a full investigation is needed (not individually verified) [1]
    - Time-sensitive matters: chronologies, patterns and hot documents to prioritise review (not individually verified) [1]
