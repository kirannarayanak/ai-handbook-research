### Security, data and AI

All five departments sit on sensitive data and on the platforms that hold it, so the strongest pattern is vendor-embedded AI that inherits existing access controls (Security Copilot, Purview, Snowflake RBAC, Power BI semantic models) and works best on well-prepared metadata. Shared risks are over-sharing through broad permissions, unverified AI output feeding decisions (alert verdicts, generated SQL, dashboard answers), and agents taking actions, so human approval gates and evaluation datasets are common requirements. Departments 47 and 48 should train together on SOC and governance handoffs and AI-agent oversight, and 49, 50 and 51 on semantic-layer preparation, answer validation and evaluation-driven development. All five should share one module on governing AI (NIST AI RMF and its generative AI profile) and on data classification. Research limit: most claims are vendor-reported, and regulatory hooks beyond NIST are general knowledge, not verified.

*Fact-check:* All eleven distinct sources opened successfully. Core vendor claims (550%, 200 hours, ISO 42001, 98%/70% figures, CORTEX_USER granted to PUBLIC, Cortex no-training, NIST AI 600-1, Power BI geographic and SKU restrictions, Foundry governance, Purview AI app coverage) were confirmed on the cited pages. Several details were overstated or unsupported and were softened or removed, most notably the Genie Code rename, KQL, one-click policies and the Purview investigation scenario. Figures remain vendor-reported, and regulatory hooks other than NIST remain unverified, so confidence stays medium. 12 correction(s) or removal(s) were applied.

**47 Cybersecurity Operations** (Specialist; medium confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Phishing triage agent in Defender autonomously handles and closes false-positive alerts; Microsoft reports SOC analysts found malicious emails up to 550% faster, and one customer quote cites nearly 200 hours saved monthly (vendor-reported) [1]
    - Incident investigation: summarise complex alerts into actionable summaries with step-by-step response guidance [1]
    - Natural-language to query-script conversion and script reverse-engineering so team members can run technical tasks (page says query-language scripts; KQL specifically not verified) [1]
    - Stakeholder incident reporting, tailored to the audience [1]
    - Agentic detection triage with parallel cross-domain investigation agents; CrowdStrike lists over 98% decision accuracy (benchmarked against its Falcon Complete MDR decisions) and 70% reduced manual effort in investigations (vendor figures) [2]
    - No-code custom security agents; actions can run autonomously or require human approval before running [2]
- **Capstone deliverable:** Triage a set of 20 anonymised or synthetic alerts with an AI assistant, producing one incident summary plus a hunting query for analyst review. Include documented prompts, a verdict-versus-analyst comparison table with errors flagged, and minutes saved per alert. Sources: vendor docs and the team's own runbook. Sized for a Specialist-tier time budget.
- **Data risks and rules:** Handles logs with personal data, credentials, indicators tied to employees and customers, and incident details that may be privileged or subject to breach notification (GDPR and similar; not verified in opened sources). Rules: use only tenant-approved tools; never paste raw incident data into consumer tools; keep humans approving containment actions; log prompts and outputs as evidence. Charlotte AI is described as ISO 42001-certified with answers traceable to source data [2].
- **Adoption KPIs:** Mean time to triage and respond; share of alerts auto-closed and later confirmed correct; analyst override rate; hours saved per month; percentage of hunting queries AI-drafted and accepted.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Spend most hours on hands-on triage and validation with Microsoft Security Copilot and CrowdStrike Charlotte AI (whichever the SOC runs), because the vendors position them for alert triage and investigation. Add Claude for query drafting and report writing on sanitised data. Teach override and approval-gate practice and error spotting rather than prompting basics.
- **Tool-list fit:** The list fits well on the tool-specific side. Gemini and Claude are general assistants and are secondary for a SOC unless wired to approved data. Google Gemini in Security was not verified in an opened source. Both opened pages show the platform agents are bound to their own ecosystem (Defender/Sentinel or Falcon), so staff should learn the one they own. Omitted: Splunk and Palo Alto AI offerings, not verified.

**48 Security Governance, Risk & Privacy** (Specialist; medium confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Discover and monitor generative AI use via Data Security Posture Management (DSPM) for AI as the front door to discover, secure and apply compliance controls for AI usage (one-click policies not verified) [1]
    - Use sensitivity labels so AI apps only return data a user may access [1]
    - Assess AI apps in use across Microsoft Copilots (incl. Security Copilot, Copilot in Fabric), Entra-registered apps, ChatGPT Enterprise and Claude Enterprise, and other AI apps [1]
    - Structure AI risk assessments around the NIST AI RMF and its Generative AI Profile (NIST-AI-600-1, July 2024) [2]
    - Security Copilot agents span Defender, Entra, Intune and Purview to help detect, investigate and respond (specific Purview data-security-investigation scenario not verified) [3]
    - Draft policy-to-control mappings and privacy workflow text for human review (Claude, Gemini) - general practice, not verified in sources
- **Capstone deliverable:** A one-page AI risk assessment for one approved AI tool, mapped to NIST AI RMF functions and the Generative AI Profile, listing data categories, label and permission checks, residual risks and owner. Include documented prompts, the sources used, and time saved against a manual assessment.
- **Data risks and rules:** Holds policies, audit evidence, privacy requests and risk registers, which can contain personal data and security weaknesses. Hooks: GDPR and other privacy law, sector rules (not verified in sources). Rules: classify before AI access; fix over-permissioned content first, since generative AI amplifies oversharing [1]; keep evidence of AI decisions; never paste vulnerability details into unapproved tools.
- **Adoption KPIs:** Share of AI tools with a completed risk assessment; percentage of sensitive content labelled; DSPM-for-AI risk findings closed; time to complete an assessment; policy exceptions raised for shadow AI.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Prioritise Microsoft Purview (labels, DSPM for AI) and Microsoft Security Copilot because they enforce controls, then ServiceNow Now Assist for workflow evidence and Claude for drafting and mapping with human review. Time goes to framework literacy (NIST) and validating outputs.
- **Tool-list fit:** Purview fits strongly and is documented as covering many AI apps [1]. ServiceNow Now Assist was not verified in an opened source. Gemini and Claude are drafting aids only. The handbook omits a framework resource such as NIST AI RMF, which is not a tool but belongs in training [2].
