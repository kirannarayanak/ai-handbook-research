### Customer and product

All five departments turn unstructured customer signals (tickets, calls, surveys, interview notes, feedback, usage data) into summaries, drafts and prioritised actions, and in each one the tool's output is a draft that a human must validate. The shared risks are customer personal data (GDPR/UK GDPR), unapproved "shadow AI" use, and confident but wrong output. Wrong output includes misread sentiment, invented user insight and mis-scored churn risk. Vendor-reported results (resolution rates, retention gains) are marketing claims and should not be used as training benchmarks. Customer Success, Support and CX should train together on voice-of-customer synthesis, data handling and human escalation rules. Product Management and Product Design & UX Research should train together on evidence-grounded PRDs, research synthesis and acceptance criteria. A joint cross-cluster session could use one shared feedback dataset, taking it from support tickets to themes to backlog items.

*Fact-check:* All nine cited URLs opened and the main statistics (Zendesk 22%, 73%, 250%, 64%, Vagaro; Intercom 51%/86%/45 languages; Qualtrics 50 brands/90%; IdeaPlan 41%/73%; NN/g 841/92%) matched the pages. Errors found were mis-attributed or unsupported claims in Product Management and UX, now removed or recitified. Vendor and practitioner-blog figures remain marketing or indicative claims. Uncited handbook-gap statements (Dovetail, Forethought, Staircase AI) are labelled not verified. 9 correction(s) or removal(s) were applied.

**36 Customer Success** (Advanced; medium confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Continuous account health monitoring: an AI agent in the CRM combines product adoption, usage velocity, support case frequency, executive engagement and contract milestones, flags anomalies and proposes actions to the CSM (tool type: CRM-embedded agent; outcome: earlier renewal-risk detection, consultancy-described, not measured) [1]
    - Next-best-action drafting: the agent recommends outreach, content to share or internal escalation, for example a technical enablement session for an account struggling with advanced features [1]
    - Routine task automation: renewal reminders, health-check emails and meeting preparation partially automated by AI so CSMs spend time on high-impact accounts [1]
    - Meeting-derived sentiment: a Gainsight customer-story page reportedly adds meeting-summary data to sentiment scoring (page could not be opened, not verified)
    - Support-signal enrichment: support-case frequency and resolution time are fed into the account profile so CSMs see service friction before a renewal conversation [1]
    - CX benchmark context for CS conversations: Zendesk reports 22% higher customer retention among 'CX Trendsetters' (vendor survey, treat as indicative) [2]
- **Capstone deliverable:** A renewal-risk brief pack for 5 anonymised or synthetic accounts. For each account: a Claude or Gemini draft health summary, a human-corrected risk rating with evidence, a success plan, and a renewal email draft. Submit with the prompt log, a source/validation table (every claim traced to a CRM field or call note) and measured time saved against a manual baseline. Sized for about 12-16 Advanced-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Customer contact details, contract values and pricing, usage telemetry, support transcripts and stakeholder names. GDPR/UK GDPR applies to named contacts and call notes (lawful basis, minimisation, DPIA for profiling). Contracts often carry confidentiality clauses. Rules: use only enterprise-approved tools; strip personal data when pasting outside the CRM; never let AI send customer-facing messages or alter commercial terms without human review; keep customer data in the CRM's governed agent where possible. Zendesk reports shadow AI use (external tools unapproved for agent use) jumping up to 250% year on year in some industries and links this to privacy, security and service-quality risk [2].
- **Adoption KPIs:** Share of at-risk accounts flagged at least 60 days before renewal; CSM prep time per QBR/renewal (minutes); percentage of AI-drafted outreach edited and approved versus rejected; gross revenue retention on accounts using AI health review versus control; weekly active AI users among CSMs.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Spend most hours on account research and synthesis, health-signal interpretation, and drafting with human review, with a short module on data handling. Prioritise Salesforce Einstein (CRM-native signals, per the Agentforce description [1]), Claude (long call and email summarisation), Microsoft 365 Copilot (meeting and email work) and Gainsight AI (the dedicated CS platform, if licensed). Gamma and Gemini can be covered briefly.
- **Tool-list fit:** The list fits well: Salesforce Einstein and Gainsight AI are the CS-native tools. Gamma is a weak fit, useful only for QBR decks. The list omits Salesforce Agentforce by name; the RSM article describes Agentforce as the renewal/health agent in Salesforce [1], so confirm whether 'Einstein' in the handbook is meant to cover it. Gainsight's Staircase AI is not on the list (not verified from an opened page).

**37 Customer Support & Contact Center** (Core; high confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Autonomous first-line resolution: Intercom's Fin, powered by Claude, reports a 51% average out-of-the-box resolution rate, up to 86% for some customers, and response times cut from 30 minutes to seconds (vendor-reported) [1]
    - Multilingual support: Fin answers in over 45 languages with a configurable tone of voice and answer length per business [1]
    - Agent copilots: Zendesk reports 73% of agents believe an AI copilot would help them do their job better, and 90% of 'CX Trendsetters' report positive returns on AI tools for agents (vendor survey) [2]
    - Zendesk customer Vagaro reports AI resolved 44% of incoming requests, cut resolution time by 87% and reached 92% CSAT (customer quote in vendor report) [2]
    - Knowledge search: Intercom's VP of AI says support reps spend a lot of time searching for authoritative answers to questions they have seen before, which AI retrieval addresses [1]
    - Ticket classification and routing: handbook use case; no opened source with measured results, so no outcome is claimed here (not verified)
- **Capstone deliverable:** A 'support assistant pack' on a synthetic or anonymised set of 30 tickets. It contains a classification scheme with AI labels checked against human labels (accuracy and error log), 10 agent-assist draft replies edited by the learner with change notes, and 3 knowledge-base article drafts. Include the documented prompts, an escalation rule list (when AI must hand off to a human) and measured handling-time saving. Sized for Core-tier hours of about 8-10.
- **Data risks and rules:** Customer PII, order and account details, payment references (PCI DSS if card data appears), sometimes health or other special-category data, and call recordings. GDPR applies to transcripts, retention and any automated decisions. Rules: mask card numbers and IDs before AI use; use only the helpdesk's governed AI, not public chatbots; disclose AI to customers and offer a human route; do not let AI promise refunds or policy exceptions. Zendesk links shadow AI use to privacy, security and service-quality risk [2]. Regulator guidance specific to contact-centre recordings was not checked, so that point is not verified.
- **Adoption KPIs:** AI resolution (containment) rate with CSAT on AI-resolved tickets; average handle time and first response time; percentage of agent drafts accepted with minor edits; knowledge-article usage and gap-closure rate; escalation-to-human rate and reopen rate.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Put most Core hours into using the helpdesk's AI (agent assist, reply drafting, KB search) with strict redaction and escalation rules. Prioritise Zendesk AI or Intercom Fin (whichever the company runs), ServiceNow Now Assist (if it is the ticketing platform), and Claude for tone editing and KB drafting. Gemini is a general fallback.
- **Tool-list fit:** Good fit overall. Zendesk AI and Intercom Fin are competing platforms, so most teams will use only one and training should follow the deployed platform. Gemini and Claude are general assistants and should be limited to redacted content. Any handbook omission of Salesforce service tooling or Zendesk's Forethought acquisition is not verified. The Intercom page shows Fin is built on Claude [1].

**38 Customer Experience & Service Design** (Advanced; medium confidence)

- **Capstone deliverable:** A voice-of-customer insight brief built from a provided set of 200 anonymised survey comments and tickets. Deliver AI-generated themes, human-verified theme counts with quote evidence, a current-state journey map with 3 pain points, and 2 service-design recommendations with an impact hypothesis. Include documented prompts, a validation log of AI-versus-human coding disagreements and time saved. Sized for about 12-16 Advanced-tier hours.

    - Story and acceptance-criteria drafting: a practitioner article reports Atlassian documentation shows Rovo generating user stories, subtasks, acceptance criteria and QA edge cases in the work-item editor [2]
    - Adoption signal: IdeaPlan's 2026 report (as cited) says 41% of product professionals use AI to generate user stories and 73% use AI tools weekly or daily; the article warns this is a synthesized signal, not an audited census [2]
    - Evidence-to-spec tools: Productboard Spark synthesises customer evidence, ranks opportunities and drafts delivery-oriented specifications [2]
    - Planning calculations: Rovo writes Jira formula fields from plain-language descriptions, for example weighted priority scores [1]
    - Space setup: Rovo can generate a Jira space with workflows, work types, views and suggested first work items from a natural-language description or attached context such as Confluence pages [1]
- **Capstone deliverable:** A one-page PRD plus a refined backlog slice for a real or sample feature. The AI-drafted PRD must be annotated with the PM's corrections, with each assumption linked to a source (customer quotes, usage data). Include 8 user stories with acceptance criteria, a release-note draft, the prompt log and a time-saved comparison. Sized for about 8-10 Core-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Unreleased roadmap and pricing (commercially confidential, possible MNPI for listed companies), customer feedback with personal data (GDPR), partner NDA material and source code excerpts. **40 Product Design & UX Research** (Advanced; medium confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Research synthesis on the canvas: Figma's AI agent summarises user feedback and interview notes, organises feedback themes and links findings to the relevant frames (vendor page) [1]
    - Design audits: the same agent audits screens for usability and accessibility issues [1]
    - Generation from the design system: Figma's agent generates and refines designs using actual components, tokens and variables, with parallel prompting and shareable threads [1]
    - Professional adoption: an NN/g survey of 841 UX professionals (July-August 2023) found 92% had used a generative AI tool (NN/g notes possible selection bias), and the most common activity was text-content generation and editing [2]
    - Four roles for generative AI in UX work: content editor, research assistant, ideation partner and design assistant [2]
    - Summarising qualitative sessions: NN/g tested AI 'insight generators' that summarise research sessions from transcripts alone, and found they miss context [3]
- **Capstone deliverable:** A usability-findings pack from 5 anonymised session transcripts or notes. Deliver AI-generated themes, the researcher's validated theme list with evidence quotes and an explicit list of what AI missed or invented, a wireframe concept with UX copy variants, and documented prompts. Include time saved and a note on which findings need real-user confirmation. Sized for about 12-16 Advanced-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Participant recordings, transcripts and demographics (GDPR/UK GDPR, consent scope and retention limits), unreleased designs and IP, and sometimes health-related data from usability sessions. Rules: check the consent form permits AI processing; anonymise before upload; keep unreleased designs inside approved tools. NN/g warns that AI insight generators do not accept product or user background and can give vague recommendations [3]; this article is from 2023 and NN/g itself notes many tools have improved since.
- **Adoption KPIs:** Synthesis time per study; share of AI-generated themes confirmed by the researcher; number of design iterations explored per sprint; usability issues found per audit; accessibility defects caught before handoff.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Advanced hours should go to research synthesis with verification, wireframe and UX-copy ideation, and critique of AI output. Prioritise Figma AI (design and audit in the canvas), FigJam AI (affinity clustering and workshops, per the handbook), Claude (transcript analysis, UX writing) and ChatGPT Enterprise or Gemini only as a second opinion.
- **Tool-list fit:** Figma AI and FigJam AI fit well. Canva Magic Studio is a weak fit for product UX, better for marketing assets. Whether the handbook omits dedicated research-repository tools is not verified; Dovetail's summarisation feature is mentioned in the NN/g survey design [2] but its current capabilities were not verified. The most important caution is NN/g's: be skeptical of marketing claims from AI research tools [3].
