### Finance, procurement and investor relations

All eight departments turn structured ledger, bank, spend or contract data into drafts, summaries and exception lists that a named human then reviews, so the shared skill is verification: reperforming numbers, tying output to source and documenting who signed off. Shared risks are confident-but-wrong output, material non-public information, personal and bank data, and weak evidence trails; sources describe COSO's February 2026 generative-AI control guidance and a Workiva finding that 1 in 4 executives said an AI error reached investors or the board. Microsoft itself warns against relying on Copilot in Excel for finance decisions. Training should be joint for a common core (data classification, review gates, prompt and evidence logging) and for three pairings: FP&A, Accounting and Treasury (variance, forecast and close narratives in spreadsheets); AP, Procurement and Vendor Management (P2P documents, contracts, supplier data and fraud signals); and FP&A with Investor Relations (earnings numbers and narrative consistency). Tax and IR need their own legal and disclosure modules.

*Fact-check:* I opened and checked 17 cited pages, including every cited page except the CPA Practice Advisor article's later sections. Statistics and named capabilities matched their sources: COSO February 2026, Workiva 1 in 4, Gartner 35%, Flowserve, AFP 74% BEC and 2.9/5 AI policy score, Hackett 47%/9.9%, TR Institute percentages, SAP Joule/Concur Verify/Bid Analysis, and the Aeries pilot. Corrections were mostly wording precision, plus flags on three tool claims no opened page supports (CoCounsel, Now Assist, Perplexity/Gamma judgments). Overall reliability is good, but the Aeries pilot and Hackett/Simfoni figures are vendor-reported. 8 correction(s) or removal(s) were applied.

**20 Financial Planning & Analysis** (Advanced; high confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Drafting first-pass variance commentary from ledger detail with an LLM; the analyst checks every claim because drafts sound equally confident when wrong [1].
    - Natural-language questions on planning data inside an FP&A platform (for example 'why did Q2 revenue drop in EMEA?') returning data-backed explanations for monthly business reviews and board books [2].
    - Running multiple what-if scenarios (inflation, tariffs, supply shifts) on driver-based models in minutes instead of days, with analysts owning assumptions [1][2].
    - Drafting management reports by department or cost centre against budget from a trial balance, then having analysts add interpretation [3].
    - Baseline statistical or ML forecasts refreshed each cycle; one source cautions that simple statistical methods have beaten ML in published tests, so baselines are a starting point [1].
- **Capstone deliverable:** A one-month variance pack for a sanitized or synthetic cost-centre dataset: AI-drafted commentary on the top 10 variances, each claim tied to a ledger line, plus a scenario table (base, downside, upside) with stated driver assumptions. Submit the documented prompts, the verification log showing corrections made to AI output, and measured time against a manual baseline. Sized for Advanced-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Unreleased results and forecasts are material non-public information for listed companies (securities law, insider-trading and Reg FD exposure). Budgets carry compensation and headcount detail (employment law, GDPR if staff-identifiable). Rules: use only enterprise-licensed tools with no training on inputs, anonymise employee-level data, keep forecast files off consumer tools, keep a human reviewer on any number that leaves finance. Microsoft's Copilot in Excel FAQ says to avoid using Copilot for decisions in sensitive areas such as finance and to verify output [4]. AFP respondents cited data reliability (61%) as a barrier [1].
- **Adoption KPIs:** Share of FP&A staff using approved AI weekly (AFP benchmark: 23% use AI daily, weekly or monthly) [1]; hours to produce monthly variance commentary versus baseline; percentage of AI-drafted claims corrected at review; forecast error by horizon before and after; number of scenarios run per planning cycle.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Spend most hours on hands-on variance commentary and scenario work with verification routines, then governance. Prioritise Excel Copilot (where the models live), Claude or ChatGPT Enterprise for narrative and long-document reasoning, and Power BI Copilot for natural-language data exploration. Teach reperformance of every number, because outputs read as authoritative even when wrong [1][4].
- **Tool-list fit:** The list fits well: Excel Copilot, Power BI Copilot and a general LLM cover the core jobs. Gemini and ChatGPT Enterprise overlap; one general assistant plus the Microsoft tools is enough for most teams (judgment). Omitted: dedicated planning platforms with embedded generative AI, such as OneStream SensibleAI, which offers narrative variance summaries and natural-language questions inside the planning model [2].

**21 Accounting & Financial Reporting** (Advanced; high confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Describing a transaction in plain English and having GenAI draft a multi-line journal entry against the chart of accounts, turning data entry into review and approval [1].
    - Uploading bank and ledger datasets so the model identifies matches, discrepancies and unrecorded items, then drafting control-account reconciliation summaries [1].
    - Estimating accruals for services received but not yet invoiced from an anonymised service contract, with accountant review [1]; SAP has released an Accounting Accruals Agent in Joule [3].
    - Variance analysis on a trial balance with first-draft commentary and draft footnote disclosures from supplied data and the relevant standard [1].
    - Close agents that prepare reconciliations and draft journals for human sign-off, from vendors such as BlackLine and FloQast [2].
- **Capstone deliverable:** A month-end mini-close on synthetic data: five reconciliations and three journal entries drafted with AI, plus draft variance commentary. Deliver a control memo covering agent scope and named owner, retained inputs and reasoning, reviewer sign-off per item, error rate found on review, and measured time saved against manual preparation. Sized for Advanced-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Unpublished financial statements (material non-public information), data feeding financial reporting under SOX internal control over financial reporting, and supplier, customer and employee personal data inside ledgers (GDPR). Rules: every AI output feeding the statements needs a human approval step scaled to materiality; keep an inventory of AI tools with a named owner; retain inputs, steps, exceptions and match basis so a reviewer or auditor can replay them; check SOC reports for what they say about AI components [2]. COSO issued guidance on internal control over generative AI in February 2026 [2]. Anonymise contracts before upload [1].
- **Adoption KPIs:** Days to close; percentage of reconciliations AI-prepared and passed at first review; reviewer-found error rate on AI drafts (set an exit threshold, as recommended) [2]; manual journal entry preparation hours; share of AI tools with a documented owner and evidence standard [2].
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Weight hours to controls and review, then to close tasks. Prioritise Excel Copilot (reconciliations, variance on trial balances), SAP Joule (where SAP is the ledger, for accruals and embedded agents), and Claude or Microsoft 365 Copilot for contract reading and journal explanations. Vendor match-rate claims are marketing until your own data reproduces them [2].
- **Tool-list fit:** Fit is good for ERP-centred teams: Excel Copilot and SAP Joule match the work, and Joule applies only if the company runs SAP [3]. Gemini adds little beyond Claude and Microsoft 365 Copilot in a Microsoft shop (judgment). Omitted: dedicated close and reconciliation platforms such as BlackLine and FloQast, plus Microsoft's Dynamics 365 reconciliation agent in preview [2].

**22 Treasury & Cash Management** (Specialist; medium confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Cash flow forecasting from daily bank balances plus open receivables and payables, producing a baseline and range and showing which regions miss most, so the treasurer reviews misses rather than collecting files [2].
    - FX exposure mapping: reading open invoices and purchase orders into currencies and settlement dates and refreshing a net exposure report daily; hedge decisions stay human [2].
    - Payment fraud screening that flags changed bank details and scores payments against vendor history, with independent release and call-back controls [2].
    - Drafting a daily cash position note from bank statements, with reviewer tie-out to bank balances [2].
    - Generative AI for cash positioning: Deloitte's 2024 survey of 213 organisations found over 70% saw cash forecasting, and 50% cash positioning, as the most impactful use cases (as summarised by HighRadius) [1].
- **Capstone deliverable:** A 13-week cash forecast pack on synthetic multi-currency data: AI-assisted baseline and range, a back-test of forecast error against actuals by horizon, a net FX exposure table, and a one-page position note. Include documented prompts, a rule list of what the tool may never do (move money, commit hedges), and time saved. Sized for Specialist-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Bank account numbers and credentials, payment instructions, covenant and liquidity positions (often material non-public information), counterparty data, and card data if in scope (PCI DSS). Business email compromise affected 74% of US organisations in AFP's 2026 payments fraud survey [2]. Rules: no bank details or credentials in prompts; AI never releases payments or changes bank mandates; dual authorisation and call-back for account changes [2]; treat AI-generated hedge or funding suggestions as drafts under the treasury policy. AFP's 2026 benchmarking survey scored AI and emerging-technology policies lowest of any treasury policy area [2], so write one first.
- **Adoption KPIs:** Forecast error by horizon versus pre-AI baseline (only 18% rated their forecasting capability above average or best in class in Deloitte's survey) [1]; hours spent assembling the weekly forecast; share of forecast files received via automated feed rather than spreadsheet; fraud flags raised and confirmed; policy exceptions logged.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Specialist hours should go to forecasting accuracy measurement and payment-control design. Prioritise Excel Copilot for scenario and position work, Power BI Copilot for liquidity reporting, and Claude for reading bank, loan and covenant documents. Include a controls module on never letting AI release funds [2].
- **Tool-list fit:** Partial fit. Excel Copilot and Power BI Copilot suit reporting, but Microsoft advises avoiding Excel Copilot for decisions in sensitive areas such as finance [3]. Gemini and ChatGPT Enterprise are generic and weaker for forecasting, which sources describe with ML or predictive tools [2]. Omitted: treasury management systems and dedicated cash-forecasting tools, such as HighRadius cash forecasting and the TMS layer, which Deloitte links to cash positioning [1].

**23 Taxation** (Specialist; medium confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Tax research: LLMs summarise complex regulations, provide precedents and answer specific queries [2]; tax research was the top GenAI use case (77%) in the Thomson Reuters Institute 2025 survey [1].
    - Extracting data from unstructured invoices, contracts and regulatory filings to cut manual entry [2].
    - Drafting first versions of tax memos, reports and responses to tax authorities for professional review [2].
    - Modelling tax planning scenarios from historical data and current rules to estimate financial impact [2].
    - Document summarisation (55%) and document review (53%) are also among top use cases in the same survey [1].
- **Capstone deliverable:** A research memo on a defined, public-fact tax question (for example a VAT treatment of a cross-border service), with every authority cited and verified against the primary source, plus a structured extraction of key fields from five sample invoices. Submit prompts, a citation verification table, an error log and time saved. Sized for Specialist-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Taxpayer identifiers, employee and shareholder data (GDPR), transfer-pricing and planning positions that are commercially sensitive and may attract legal privilege if prepared with counsel, and unfiled results (material non-public information). Rules: cite and verify every authority since accuracy and reliability are the main reservations among tax professionals [1]; keep privileged advice inside approved, access-controlled tools; set governance for data security, access controls and audit trails as the insightsoftware guide recommends [2]; never let AI submit filings. Confirm local rules on tax-data handling with counsel (not verified in sources).
- **Adoption KPIs:** Research hours per query versus baseline; share of AI-cited authorities verified correct; documents processed per hour with extraction accuracy; percentage of GenAI users using it at least weekly (73% of tax-firm and 69% of corporate tax users in the 2025 survey) [1]; whether ROI is measured at all, since only 20% of professionals surveyed do [1].
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Spend hours on research verification and extraction QA. Prioritise Thomson Reuters CoCounsel (grounded tax research; not verified in opened sources), Claude for long-document reading and memo drafting, and Excel Copilot for scenario and reconciliation work. Teach hallucinated-citation checks first.
- **Tool-list fit:** Good fit: a research-grade tool such as CoCounsel (not verified in opened sources) with general LLMs for drafting. Gemini is generic and weaker for authoritative research (judgment). Omitted: tax-technology suites with embedded AI, such as ONESOURCE (Thomson Reuters) and insightsoftware's Longview Tax, both named in the opened pages [1][2].

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Extracting, classifying and validating fields from unstructured invoice PDFs with Azure AI Document Intelligence, with human-in-the-loop exception handling; a vendor-reported three-month healthcare proof of concept claimed over 70% field accuracy and about 60% less processing time (self-reported, no baseline disclosed) [1].

    - Receipt analysis and expense checks: SAP Concur Verify inspects receipt images and metadata to flag likely AI-generated receipts [2].
    - Payment-fraud screening that flags changed supplier bank details and scores payments against vendor history, with call-back controls [3].
    - Drafting collection follow-ups and supplier queries on exceptions, always sent after human review (judgment, not source-verified).
- **Capstone deliverable:** An exception-handling playbook tested on 30 synthetic invoices (price mismatch, missing PO, duplicate, changed bank details): AI extraction plus a triage table recommending approve, query supplier or escalate, with accuracy scored against an answer key. - **Data risks and rules:** Supplier bank details and tax IDs, customer payment data (PCI DSS if card data is present), employee expense data (GDPR), and entries that feed SOX-relevant controls. Omitted: dedicated invoice capture and AP automation (the pilot used Azure AI Document Intelligence) [1] and ERP-native tools. Claude and Gemini are secondary for extraction at volume (judgment).

**27 Investor Relations** (Advanced; high confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Feeding a draft script or earnings into an AI tool to generate sample analyst questions for CFO rehearsal [1][2].
    - First-draft prepared remarks from a bot trained on a CFO's past public remarks, which the CFO then red-lines (Expensify's in-house tool) [2].
    - Competitive intelligence and peer benchmarking: analysing how peers discuss topics such as FX in their disclosures [1][2].
    - Compliance support for regulatory disclosure review before release [1].
    - Anticipating more complex investor questions as analysts use AI themselves; at least 35% of CFOs polled by Gartner reported higher volume, frequency and urgency of investor communications in 2025 versus 2024 [2].
- **Capstone deliverable:** An earnings-prep kit built on a public company's already-released results: AI-generated list of 25 analyst questions ranked by difficulty, draft answers checked against filed figures, a peer-language comparison, and a Reg FD-style review checklist. Submit prompts, a source-verification table, items AI got wrong and time saved. Use only public data. Sized for Advanced-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Unreleased earnings, guidance and strategic plans are material non-public information; Regulation FD bars selective disclosure, and the 2005 Flowserve action cited the IR officer alongside the CEO [3]. Rules: AI never decides what is material or compliant; use only enterprise tools with no training on inputs; draft Q&A inside the restricted insider list; legal and disclosure committee review before anything external; one in four executives surveyed by Workiva (2026 mid-year report) said an AI-generated error had reached investors or the board [2]. Consistency with filed numbers must be checked by a person.
- **Adoption KPIs:** Hours of earnings-prep time versus prior quarter; share of analyst questions on the call that were anticipated in the AI-assisted Q&A; number of numeric or factual errors caught before release; percentage of drafts passing disclosure-committee review at first pass; AI usage logged per release cycle.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Hours go to Q&A rehearsal, narrative consistency and disclosure controls. Prioritise Claude or ChatGPT Enterprise for question generation and drafting, Microsoft 365 Copilot for working within Excel and PowerPoint source files, and Power BI Copilot for KPI narratives. Include a module on MNPI and the review chain.

Omitted: market-intelligence platforms such as AlphaSense, whose IR use cases cover script drafting, peer benchmarking and compliance/disclosure support [1], and which Bloomberg Tax describes as extracting details from filings and earnings transcripts for investors [2]; Workiva for disclosure workflows (Workiva named in [2] as a compliance platform).
