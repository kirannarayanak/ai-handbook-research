### Legal, audit and GRC AI

These three tools are vertical specialists anchored to a proprietary corpus or system of record rather than general assistants: CoCounsel sells agentic AI on top of Thomson Reuters' Westlaw, Practical Law and Checkpoint content; Datasite embeds AI inside the virtual data room where deal documents already sit; Optro (formerly AuditBoard) embeds agents inside the GRC system of record. They barely overlap with one another, but all three compete for budget and attention with horizontal assistants such as Claude, ChatGPT and Microsoft 365 Copilot, and each has shipped or announced MCP connectors in 2026 so corporate instances of those assistants can reach their governed content and workflows under existing permissions. Enterprises typically standardise on one platform per function (legal research/drafting, deal rooms, GRC) and pair it with a horizontal assistant, because switching costs are driven by the underlying content subscriptions, deal archives or control libraries rather than the AI layer. Pricing is contract-based with no transparent enterprise list price except CoCounsel's self-serve small-firm tiers. The shared governance pattern is explicit no-training commitments, security attestations (details differ per vendor and some are verified only on vendor pages noted per tool), and citation traceability with human approval as the primary control.

*Fact-check:* Vendor pricing pages were re-opened for CoCounsel (the three starting prices match exactly), Datasite (quote-only confirmed) and Optro (no published prices). The headline no-training, renaming and ISO 42001 claims were confirmed on vendor pages. Many granular security attestations (CoCounsel SOC 2/ISO/FedRAMP and OpenAI/Google subprocessor details, Optro SOC 1/TX-RAMP/HIPAA report type) and several specific dates and counts could not be found on the pages I reopened, so they were removed or marked not verified, and confidence is lowered to medium for all three tools. 9 correction(s) or removal(s) were applied.
