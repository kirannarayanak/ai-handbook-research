### Sales, marketing and business development

All eight departments use AI for the same core jobs: research and synthesis, personalised first drafts, call and meeting summarisation, and reporting on CRM or campaign data. Sellers and marketers also report that data quality, not the model, is the limiting factor (Salesforce: 74% of AI-using sales teams prioritise data hygiene). Shared risks are personal data of prospects and contacts (UK/EU GDPR, PECR or e-privacy marketing consent), recorded-call consent (wiretap litigation against AI notetakers), confidential pipeline, pricing and customer data, and unverified AI claims reaching buyers. Vendor-reported gains are large but self-reported, and independent analyses of ad automation are more mixed, so training should teach measuring incremental results against a baseline. Train together on a common module covering CRM data hygiene, consent and recording rules, citation-checked research and a human review gate before external sending. Sales Ops with the two sales teams, Marketing with Digital Marketing, and Research with BD and Pre-Sales are natural pairings for hands-on labs.

*Fact-check:* I opened nearly all cited pages and most headline statistics (Salesforce 34%/36%/48%/55%/1.7x/74%/79% vs 54%/51%, Gong 130% and 1.8M opportunities, Verse.ai 25%, Cox 55%, Anthropic 30 minutes to 30 seconds, Qualtrics 71%/87%, Haus 640 tests, ICO PECR and controller/processor text) match their sources. One use case (Cyera registrations) and the SOX claim had no support and were removed or flagged; the ICO "absolute right to object to profiling" wording was corrected to what the page says. Several tool-fit claims (Einstein, NotebookLM, Canva, Power BI Copilot, RFP platforms) remain unverified and are labelled so. Vendor-authored statistics are self-reported, and departments 34 and 35 stay low confidence because their evidence is thin. 9 correction(s) or removal(s) were applied.

**28 Sales - Enterprise & Key Accounts** (Core; medium confidence)

- **AI use cases in practice:**
    - Account and prospect research summarised from CRM, web and internal data; sellers expect agents to cut research time by 34% once fully implemented (survey of 4,050 sellers) [1]
    - Call summaries, next-step capture and multi-threading checks: Gong Labs found multi-threaded deals over $50K have 130% higher win rates, so AI flags single-threaded deals [2]
    - Meeting prep and at-risk deal queries in a copilot grounded in CRM, email and decks, e.g. 'a list of deals that are at risk of falling through' [4]
    - Professional-services firm pilot: early Copilot users reported about 3 hours saved per week, with CRM-based opportunity use cases [3]
    - Personalised outreach drafts reviewed by the rep; Salesforce reports agents contacted 130,000 leads and created 3,200 opportunities in four months [1]
- **Capstone deliverable:** One strategic-account plan for a real or anonymised key account: AI-assisted account brief with every fact linked to a validated source, a stakeholder map flagging single-threaded risk, a call-summary-to-next-steps workflow, a prompt log, and a time-saved comparison (manual vs AI-assisted, minutes per task). Sized for Core-tier hours.
- **Data risks and rules:** Customer confidential data, pricing, contract terms and pipeline are commercially sensitive; contact data is personal data under GDPR/UK GDPR. Call recordings need participant consent and notice: AI notetakers face wiretap-law class actions (federal and state wiretapping laws; e.g. Brewer v. Otter.ai, N.D. Cal. 2025) [5]. Rules: use only the enterprise-approved tool, announce recording, no pasting named customer data into consumer chatbots, human review before anything is sent.
- **Adoption KPIs:** Weekly active AI users per rep; minutes spent on pre-call research and CRM updates (before vs after); share of opportunities with 3+ buyer contacts; calls recorded with consent notice logged (%); follow-up email turnaround time.
- **Curriculum emphasis:** Spend most hours on account research with citation checks, call-summary workflows, and CRM-grounded prompting. Prioritise Salesforce Einstein and Gong AI because they work on the CRM and call data sellers already use; Microsoft 365 Copilot for email and meeting prep; Claude for synthesis. Teach consent and verification explicitly; Gartner-style reinvestment of saved time is a manager topic.
- **Tool-list fit:** Good fit overall: Einstein, Gong and Copilot match the cited use cases [1][2][4]. Gamma is a weak fit for core selling work (deck output, not research or CRM). Omission: AI meeting notetakers such as Otter and Fireflies are widely used and Otter is the subject of 2025 consent litigation, so the handbook should name the approved recorder and ban unapproved bots [5]. Sales agents in Microsoft 365 Copilot connect to Dynamics 365 and Salesforce, relevant if the firm is on either CRM [4]. Einstein and Gong capabilities beyond the cited pages: not verified.
