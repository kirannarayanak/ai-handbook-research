General AI assistants and research tools

These six are horizontal assistants rather than departmental systems. Gemini, Claude and ChatGPT Enterprise compete as general chat-plus-agent platforms, while NotebookLM (now Gemini Notebook) and Perplexity specialise in grounded research: the former over uploaded documents, the latter over the live web with citations. Overlap is growing: the big assistants offer deep research, file creation, connectors and agents; Perplexity offers third-party models (GPT, Gemini, Claude) inside its own interface. Enterprises usually standardise on the assistant bundled with their productivity suite, add one frontier chat platform for knowledge workers, and buy Perplexity Enterprise or Gemini Notebook as a niche research tool. Key procurement differences: training defaults (enterprise tiers exclude training by default; consumer tiers of Gemini, Claude and Perplexity differ), compliance coverage (varies by tier and sub-feature) and pricing model (flat seats versus seat-plus-usage). The claim that Gemini Enterprise routes work to Claude is not verified on an opened page.

Microsoft Copilots and Power Platform

All six tools are Microsoft products that share one identity, permission and compliance plane (Entra ID, Microsoft Graph, Purview), but they sit on two different licensing stacks. Microsoft 365 Copilot (now marketed as "Microsoft Copilot") is a per-user add-on, and Copilot in Word, Excel and Teams are not separate products but surfaces of that single licence; the free Copilot Chat tier gives enterprise-data-protected chat without the in-app features. Power BI Copilot and Power Automate belong to the Fabric/Power Platform stack, which is priced by capacity (Fabric CU) or by maker/bot licence plus consumption-based Copilot Credits, so the "Copilot" label hides very different cost models. Overlap is real: Teams recap competes with Teams Premium, Excel Copilot and Power BI Copilot both answer data questions, and Power Automate cloud flows compete with Copilot Studio agent flows. Enterprises already on Microsoft 365 E3/E5 usually standardise on the Copilot add-on (or the new E7 bundle) for knowledge workers, Power BI on Fabric capacity for analytics, and Power Automate Premium plus a governed Centre of Excellence for automation, while using Purview and DLP as the common control layer.

Presentation, design and document tools

These six tools form the output layer on top of general assistants and office suites: they turn text, data and documents into decks, graphics, prototypes and document summaries. Gamma and Canva compete for prompt-to-presentation and on-brand collateral (Gamma launched Gamma Imagine in March 2026 to take on Canva and Adobe, per TechCrunch). Coding and engineering assistants

These six tools span IDE assistants (GitHub Copilot, Gemini Code Assist, Amazon Q Developer), an AI-native editor (Cursor), a terminal/cloud coding agent (Claude Code) and the open-source test framework (Playwright) that all of them now drive through MCP servers and agent definitions. They compete head-on in agentic multi-file coding, automated PR review and enterprise controls, and increasingly resell the same frontier models: Copilot and Cursor offer Claude, GPT and Gemini, while Claude Code itself runs inside VS Code and Cursor and on Bedrock, Google Cloud Agent Platform and Microsoft Foundry. 2026 was a consolidation year: Google stopped selling new Code Assist subscriptions (Oct 9) in favour of Antigravity, AWS announced Q Developer's end of support for Kiro (Apr 30 2027), SpaceX folded Cursor into a Grok/X bundle (Oct 12), and GitHub moved to usage-metered AI Credits (June 1). Enterprises typically standardise on GitHub Copilot where GitHub is already the SCM, add Claude Code or Cursor for agentic work, keep the hyperscaler assistant matching their primary cloud, and adopt Playwright with its MCP server as the neutral QA layer. Procurement now has to track usage-based metering and model-provider retention terms as closely as seat prices.

Enterprise business platforms

These six platforms embed AI inside the system of record each vendor already owns: ServiceNow for IT, employee and customer service plus GRC, Salesforce for CRM, SAP for ERP/procurement/supply chain, Workday for HCM and finance, Atlassian for work management, and UiPath for automation that crosses systems. They overlap increasingly at the edges: ServiceNow, Workday, Salesforce and SAP all now sell employee self-service agents and cross-vendor agent governance (AI Control Tower, Agent System of Record, Agentforce 360, SAP AI Agent Hub), and ServiceNow, Workday, UiPath and Atlassian expose MCP-based action layers so third-party assistants such as Claude or Copilot can act inside them. Enterprises typically standardise on the AI of whichever platform holds the data (ServiceNow for ITSM, Salesforce for CRM, SAP for ERP, Workday for HR, Jira for engineering/PMO) and use UiPath where work spans systems, rather than buying a separate assistant per platform. The common 2025-2026 shift is from per-seat add-ons to consumption metering (assists, Flex Credits, AI Units, Rovo credits, Platform Units), so budget governance, output review and admin controls are the shared training themes. ISO/IEC 42001 certification was confirmed for ServiceNow, Salesforce, SAP, Workday and UiPath; it was not verified for Atlassian.

Sales and marketing AI

The six tools form three layers. Gong is conversation and revenue intelligence that sits on top of the CRM; HubSpot's Breeze Assistant and Agent Hub are AI native to the CRM itself; LinkedIn's Sales Navigator and Hiring Assistant monetise LinkedIn's member graph for prospecting and recruiting; Google Ads AI (AI Max, Performance Max, Ask Advisor) and Meta Advantage+ are opaque optimisation suites inside the two dominant ad platforms. Gong, HubSpot and Sales Navigator overlap on account research and outreach drafting and are increasingly wired together (Gong exposes MCP servers to HubSpot and Microsoft 365 Copilot; Sales Navigator Advanced Plus syncs into HubSpot, Salesforce, Dynamics and Oracle). Google and Meta compete for the same performance budgets and are converging on "give us a URL and a budget" automation while removing manual controls. Enterprises usually standardise on one CRM-native assistant, add Gong for enterprise sales coaching and forecasting, licence LinkedIn seats to sales and talent teams, and treat Google and Meta AI as unavoidable defaults that marketers must learn to steer rather than tools they choose.

Customer service and experience AI

All four tools sit on the post-sale side of the customer lifecycle but on different layers. Zendesk AI and Fin compete head-on for autonomous ticket resolution and agent-assist in the contact centre: both now bill per outcome or resolution as well as (Zendesk) per seat, and both can run on rival help desks (Forethought AI Agents by Zendesk on Salesforce or Freshworks; Fin on Zendesk, Salesforce, HubSpot or Freshdesk). Gainsight works upstream in customer success, mining emails, meetings and tickets for renewal risk and expansion signals, while Qualtrics spans the whole journey, measuring experience and increasingly acting on it through Experience Agents. The 2026 consolidation wave reshapes vendor choice: Salesforce closed its acquisition of Fin on 10 September 2026, Zendesk completed its Forethought purchase in March 2026, and Qualtrics' $6.75B Press Ganey Forsta acquisition (May 2026) was confirmed in Qualtrics' own release. CRM and help-desk allegiance now determines which AI is practical to standardise on. Enterprises typically standardise on one help-desk AI (Zendesk or Fin/Intercom), Gainsight or CRM-native tooling for success, and Qualtrics for voice-of-customer, then connect signals through CRM integrations or MCP. (The ~$3.6B Fin price was not re-verified from an opened page.)

Legal, audit and GRC AI

These three tools are vertical specialists anchored to a proprietary corpus or system of record rather than general assistants: CoCounsel sells agentic AI on top of Thomson Reuters' Westlaw, Practical Law and Checkpoint content; Datasite embeds AI inside the virtual data room where deal documents already sit; Optro (formerly AuditBoard) embeds agents inside the GRC system of record. They barely overlap with one another, but all three compete for budget and attention with horizontal assistants such as Claude, ChatGPT and Microsoft 365 Copilot, and each has shipped or announced MCP connectors in 2026 so corporate instances of those assistants can reach their governed content and workflows under existing permissions. Enterprises typically standardise on one platform per function (legal research/drafting, deal rooms, GRC) and pair it with a horizontal assistant, because switching costs are driven by the underlying content subscriptions, deal archives or control libraries rather than the AI layer. Pricing is contract-based with no transparent enterprise list price except CoCounsel's self-serve small-firm tiers. The shared governance pattern is explicit no-training commitments, security attestations (details differ per vendor and some are verified only on vendor pages noted per tool), and citation traceability with human approval as the primary control.

Security, cloud operations and observability AI

These seven tools are embedded, platform-native assistants and agents rather than general chat tools, and each is only as good as the telemetry it sits on. Microsoft Security Copilot, CrowdStrike Charlotte AI and Google Gemini in Security compete directly as SOC triage and investigation agents (departments 45/47). In all seven the pricing model is consumption or bundle based (SCUs, credits, commitments, ingestion), and the dominant 2025-26 trend is a move from chat assistants to semi-autonomous agents that need human-approval guardrails.

Data, analytics and AI development platforms
