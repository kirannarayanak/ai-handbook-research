Reference

Questions everyone asks first

Plain answers about how to use the site, what the labels mean, and where to begin.

Is this an official document from the tool vendors?

No. It is an independent research companion to the Corporate AI Tools & Training Handbook. Every fact links to the page a researcher opened, often a vendor page, but vendors did not write or approve it.

I have never used an AI tool. Where do I start?

Read the first chapter of the learning path (Foundations, about eight minutes). Then open your department page and follow the 'What to learn, in order' list. Step one is always the one general assistant your company has approved.

Which tool should I learn first?

The general assistant your company already licenses: Microsoft Copilot, Gemini, Claude or ChatGPT Enterprise. Learn that one well. Everything else on your department page comes after it, and specialist tools only matter if your organisation runs that platform.

What do the confidence labels mean?

They are the fact-checker's rating of how well a page could be verified. High: two or more independent sources agree. Medium: the core facts are verified but some details are not, or the only sources are the vendor's own. Low: a key page could not be opened or only one source exists. A low rating does not mean the tool is bad; it means check before you rely on the detail.

What does 'not verified' mean?

A researcher could not open the page that would prove the claim, so it came from a search snippet or an earlier run. It is kept only where useful and always labelled. Treat it as a lead, not a fact.

Why do some tools have different names here than in the handbook?

Vendors renamed or retired at least fourteen tools between the handbook's writing and this research. Pages use the handbook name with the current name explained; the Gaps and renames page lists them all.

How are the hours estimated?

From the handbook's tier bands (Core 6-10, Advanced 12-20, Specialist 20-40+ hours per tool) and each tool's capability surface. They are planning ranges, not measured training times. The shared foundation module is counted once.

Can I put company data into these tools?

Only into the enterprise plan your company has approved, and only the kinds of data your policy allows. Never into free or personal accounts. The Foundations chapter explains the three tiers; each tool page has an 'Enterprise data handling' section.

Are the courses and certifications free?

Many vendor foundation courses are free (for example Anthropic's Claude 101, HubSpot Academy, Google Skillshop). Formal certification exams usually cost money. Each entry shows the cost as the vendor page stated it, or 'not stated' if it did not.

How current is this?

Research ran on 24-25 October 2026. AI products change monthly, so prices, names and availability may have moved. The Gaps page lists what had already changed by then.

How do I use the matrix?

Start in the high-level view: rows are department clusters, columns are tool families, numbers are how many recommendations fall in each cell. Switch to the detailed grid to see every department against every tool, then click a cell to open either page.

How do I compare tools?

Open Compare, type a tool name to add it (up to four), or click a preset such as 'General assistants'. The table lines up pricing, data handling, confidence and department fit. The URL updates so you can share the comparison.
