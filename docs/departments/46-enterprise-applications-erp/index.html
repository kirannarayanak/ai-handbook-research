AI use cases in practice

How teams like this actually use AI today, each with its source.

  • Conversational assistance inside SAP S/4HANA, SuccessFactors, Ariba and Customer Experience; the article says finance users can request margin forecasts and supply chain managers can simulate disruptions [1].
  • Procurement: summarising supplier risk, generating RFQs and initiating approvals in Ariba through Joule [1].
  • Customer-dispute handling; a European auto parts supplier reportedly cut time-to-resolve in S/4HANA by 40% (vendor-sourced, unaudited, unnamed customer) [1].
  • HR service automation; a global consumer goods company reportedly automates 60% of HR service requests with Joule (vendor-sourced, unnamed customer) [1].
  • ERP migration and implementation support; Joule was trained on SAP consulting assets to assist ERP implementations (2024 announcement) [2].
  • Cross-assistant integration, with Joule to be integrated with Microsoft Copilot for 365 and Google Cloud's Gemini (2024 announcement) [2].

Capstone deliverable

The one real piece of work a learner produces to pass. It follows the handbook's assignment template.

Within about 16 hours: for one ERP or CRM process (for example supplier dispute handling), deliver a documented AI-assisted workflow with a role-and-permission map, ten validated test prompts checked against system-of-record data, an exception log of wrong or unauthorised answers, and a before-and-after time measurement.

Data risks and rules

The sensitive data this team handles, the law that applies, and the rules that follow.

ERP and CRM systems hold financial records (SOX), personal data of customers and employees (GDPR, employment law), supplier pricing and possibly material non-public information. Rules: AI must respect existing role-based permissions, outputs must be checked against the system of record before posting, and no records go into unsanctioned tools. ERP Today states Joule agent activity is governed and auditable via SAP LeanIX (a vendor-friendly claim) [1]. Gartner flags shadow AI, vendor lock-in and data sovereignty as blind spots [3].

Adoption KPIs

Numbers to track so you know whether the training changed anything.

1) Share of target tasks completed through the embedded assistant. 2) Time-to-resolve for disputes or support requests versus baseline. 3) Error rate: AI answers corrected on validation. 4) Access or permission violations detected in AI interactions. 5) Active users per licence (%).

Curriculum emphasis

Where the hours should go, and which tools to prioritise.

Focus on embedded, permission-aware assistants and on validating outputs against the system of record, because a wrong ERP action has financial consequences. Prioritise SAP Joule (embedded in S/4HANA, Ariba) and Microsoft 365 Copilot (documentation, with Joule integration noted [2]); Salesforce Einstein (CRM) is not verified from an opened source. Use Claude or Gemini mainly for functional specs and test scripts.

Tool-list fit

Whether the handbook's tool list suits this team, and what it misses.

SAP Joule fits, but only helps teams running SAP, so training should be tailored to the company's actual stack. Salesforce Einstein's fit is not verified from an opened source. The list omits Oracle, Workday AI (listed for HR departments but not here), and Dynamics-type suites; I did not verify any omitted tool for this function. Customer outcomes found are vendor-sourced, so confidence is limited.

Jargon on this page

  • AgentAn AI that does not just answer but takes actions: runs code, opens tickets, sends messages, edits systems. Agents need an approval step for anything that cannot be undone.
  • PromptThe instruction or question you give an AI tool. Clear prompts with context and a desired format get better results.
  • Shadow AIStaff using AI tools the company has not approved, often personal accounts, which can leak confidential data.
  • ConfidenceHow well a fact-checker could verify a page. High: two or more independent sources agree. Medium: core facts verified, details not. Low: a key page could not be opened or only one source exists.
  • VerifiedVerified means a researcher opened the page and read the claim on it. Not verified means it came from a search snippet or a page that would not load, so treat it with care.
  • CopilotMicrosoft's name for its AI assistants inside Word, Excel, Teams, Power BI, security tools and Azure. One licence covers several of them.
  • GDPRThe EU's data protection law. Any AI use involving information about identifiable people has to respect it.
