AI use cases in practice

How teams like this actually use AI today, each with its source.

  • Autonomous alert investigation: Datadog Bits AI SRE analyses runbooks and telemetry, forms hypothetical root causes, validates its findings and delivers a conclusion to collaboration tools before on-call staff log in (vendor claim: hours to minutes). [1]
  • At Nulab, a small SRE team uses Bits Investigation across logs, metrics and traces; many low-priority alerts that took at least 30 minutes can now be triaged in under 5 minutes. This is a vendor-published case study. [2]
  • Natural-language investigation across infrastructure and costs, replacing hand-written queries. [2]
  • Verification loops in Claude Code: running a check the agent can read, such as a test or build. Applying this to runbook and pipeline scripts is this handbook's extrapolation. [3]
  • Runbook drafting by a general assistant (not verified in the sources); Bits posts its conclusion in collaboration tools. [1]

Capstone deliverable

The one real piece of work a learner produces to pass. It follows the handbook's assignment template.

Using a past incident (sanitised), have an AI assistant draft a runbook and an investigation timeline. Compare its root-cause hypothesis with the actual post-mortem, and deliver the runbook, the prompt log, an accuracy assessment (hits, misses, unsupported claims) and time saved.

Data risks and rules

The sensitive data this team handles, the law that applies, and the rules that follow.

Logs and traces can contain personal data (GDPR), health data (HIPAA) and credentials; incident records may contain security-sensitive detail. Rules: redact before sharing outside the observability platform; use role-based access (Datadog advertises RBAC and HIPAA-regulated workload support for Bits AI SRE [1]); AI conclusions are hypotheses requiring human confirmation before changes to production.

Adoption KPIs

Numbers to track so you know whether the training changed anything.

Mean time to acknowledge and to resolve; share of alerts with an AI investigation accepted as correct; false-hypothesis rate; runbook coverage of top alert types; on-call hours saved.

Curriculum emphasis

Where the hours should go, and which tools to prioritise.

Specialist tier: hours on incident-style simulations. Prioritise the observability-native agent (Datadog Bits AI) and Claude Code for runbooks and scripts, plus GitHub Copilot for pipeline files (not verified). Train on confirming AI root causes rather than trusting them.

Tool-list fit

Whether the handbook's tool list suits this team, and what it misses.

Datadog and Dynatrace are each relevant only if the company uses them; teach the one in use. Dynatrace Davis AI could not be verified from an opened page. Gemini Code Assist as a weaker fit for incident work is the author's judgement (not verified); check its new-subscription availability. Evidence is mostly vendor-published.

Jargon on this page

  • AI assistantA tool you talk to in plain language (Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot). It drafts, summarises and answers, but it can be wrong, so you check its work.
  • AgentAn AI that does not just answer but takes actions: runs code, opens tickets, sends messages, edits systems. Agents need an approval step for anything that cannot be undone.
  • PromptThe instruction or question you give an AI tool. Clear prompts with context and a desired format get better results.
  • TierThe handbook's training depth: Core (6-10 hours per tool), Advanced (12-20), Specialist (20-40+). A department's tier sets how long to budget.
  • VerifiedVerified means a researcher opened the page and read the claim on it. Not verified means it came from a search snippet or a page that would not load, so treat it with care.
  • Vendor claimA figure published by the company that sells the tool. It may be true, but no one independent has checked it.
  • Training on your dataWhether the vendor uses what you type to improve its AI model. Business and enterprise plans almost always say no by default; free consumer plans often say yes.
  • CopilotMicrosoft's name for its AI assistants inside Word, Excel, Teams, Power BI, security tools and Azure. One licence covers several of them.
