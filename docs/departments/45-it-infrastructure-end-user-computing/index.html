AI use cases in practice

How teams like this actually use AI today, each with its source.

  • Ticket deflection through AI self-service; ServiceNow's internal deployments reportedly reached deflection of up to about 54% on common issue-reporting forms, vendor-reported and cited second-hand [1].
  • Resolved tickets turned into draft knowledge articles, with the AI identifying which existing article to update and a human reviewing before publication [1].
  • Semantic search or RAG over the knowledge base so users can describe a problem in plain language [1].
  • Agentic resolution of requests across several tools; the article warns that much vendor 'agentic AI' is still advisory GenAI [1].
  • Estimating the blast radius of a planned change from a clean CMDB and composite AI before release [1].
  • Drafting change communications and impact summaries for stakeholders using GenAI, grounded by causal AI against CMDB topology [1].

Capstone deliverable

The one real piece of work a learner produces to pass. It follows the handbook's assignment template.

Within about 8 hours: audit the 20 most-requested end-user issues, then deliver five AI-drafted knowledge articles reviewed by a senior technician. Include the prompt log, a staleness check on existing articles, and a deflection report using the 'full AI resolution, no recontact within 72 hours' definition, plus estimated time saved per ticket.

Data risks and rules

The sensitive data this team handles, the law that applies, and the rules that follow.

Tickets and endpoint logs contain employee personal data (GDPR, employment law), credentials, device and network details, and sometimes regulated data. Rules: redact identifiers before prompting, use only enterprise-approved tools, and require human approval for irreversible autonomous actions. The source names data leakage, prompt injection and irreversible autonomous actions as the consistent production risks [1], and warns that a knowledge-base-grounded agent can expose documents users should not see [1]. Gartner found 69% of organizations suspect or confirm prohibited public GenAI use (survey of 302 cybersecurity leaders) [2].

Adoption KPIs

Numbers to track so you know whether the training changed anything.

1) True AI resolution rate (no recontact in 72 hours), not dashboard deflection; the source says true rates often run 15-25% below vendor dashboards [1]. 2) Mean time to resolve for AI-assisted tickets. 3) Knowledge articles created from tickets and share reviewed within SLA. 4) Stale article share in the knowledge base. 5) Employee CSAT after AI-handled contacts.

Curriculum emphasis

Where the hours should go, and which tools to prioritise.

Spend most hours on knowledge-base hygiene and grounding, since the source calls it the largest predictor of AI ITSM ROI [1], then on safe use of agents with approval gates. Prioritise ServiceNow Now Assist (ticket workflow), Microsoft 365 Copilot (documentation and communications), and Claude or Gemini (article drafting and troubleshooting analysis); these tool names are not covered by the cited sources. Add a short module on shadow AI policy [2].

Tool-list fit

Whether the handbook's tool list suits this team, and what it misses.

ServiceNow Now Assist fits the service-desk workflow (the source mentions ServiceNow only for the deflection figure, not Now Assist features). Microsoft Security Copilot is a weaker fit for routine end-user support, because it targets security operations (this is my judgement, not stated in an opened source). The list names no end-user device-management or non-ServiceNow ITSM AI tooling; I did not verify specific omitted products. Caveat: the main source is written by the CEO of a competing AI-native ITSM vendor (Ravenna), so its framing of vendors is not neutral.

Jargon on this page

  • AgentAn AI that does not just answer but takes actions: runs code, opens tickets, sends messages, edits systems. Agents need an approval step for anything that cannot be undone.
  • PromptThe instruction or question you give an AI tool. Clear prompts with context and a desired format get better results.
  • Prompt injectionA trick where text inside an email, web page or document secretly instructs an AI agent to do something harmful. It is why agents should not act on content you do not trust.
  • Shadow AIStaff using AI tools the company has not approved, often personal accounts, which can leak confidential data.
  • Vendor claimA figure published by the company that sells the tool. It may be true, but no one independent has checked it.
  • RAGRetrieval-augmented generation: the AI looks up your documents first and answers from them, which reduces but does not remove errors.
  • CopilotMicrosoft's name for its AI assistants inside Word, Excel, Teams, Power BI, security tools and Azure. One licence covers several of them.
  • SOCSecurity Operations Centre: the team that watches for cyber attacks. SIEM is the software that collects their security logs.
