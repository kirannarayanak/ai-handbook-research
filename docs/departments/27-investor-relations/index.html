AI use cases in practice

How teams like this actually use AI today, each with its source.

  • Feeding a draft script or earnings into an AI tool to generate sample analyst questions for CFO rehearsal [1][2].
  • First-draft prepared remarks from a bot trained on a CFO's past public remarks, which the CFO then red-lines (Expensify's in-house tool) [2].
  • Competitive intelligence and peer benchmarking: analysing how peers discuss topics such as FX in their disclosures [1][2].
  • Compliance support for regulatory disclosure review before release [1].
  • Anticipating more complex investor questions as analysts use AI themselves; at least 35% of CFOs polled by Gartner reported higher volume, frequency and urgency of investor communications in 2025 versus 2024 [2].

Capstone deliverable

The one real piece of work a learner produces to pass. It follows the handbook's assignment template.

An earnings-prep kit built on a public company's already-released results: AI-generated list of 25 analyst questions ranked by difficulty, draft answers checked against filed figures, a peer-language comparison, and a Reg FD-style review checklist. Submit prompts, a source-verification table, items AI got wrong and time saved. Use only public data. Sized for Advanced-tier hours.

Data risks and rules

The sensitive data this team handles, the law that applies, and the rules that follow.

Unreleased earnings, guidance and strategic plans are material non-public information; Regulation FD bars selective disclosure, and the 2005 Flowserve action cited the IR officer alongside the CEO [3]. Rules: AI never decides what is material or compliant; use only enterprise tools with no training on inputs; draft Q&A inside the restricted insider list; legal and disclosure committee review before anything external; one in four executives surveyed by Workiva (2026 mid-year report) said an AI-generated error had reached investors or the board [2]. Consistency with filed numbers must be checked by a person.

Adoption KPIs

Numbers to track so you know whether the training changed anything.

Hours of earnings-prep time versus prior quarter; share of analyst questions on the call that were anticipated in the AI-assisted Q&A; number of numeric or factual errors caught before release; percentage of drafts passing disclosure-committee review at first pass; AI usage logged per release cycle.

Curriculum emphasis

Where the hours should go, and which tools to prioritise.

Hours go to Q&A rehearsal, narrative consistency and disclosure controls. Prioritise Claude or ChatGPT Enterprise for question generation and drafting, Microsoft 365 Copilot for working within Excel and PowerPoint source files, and Power BI Copilot for KPI narratives. Include a module on MNPI and the review chain.

Tool-list fit

Whether the handbook's tool list suits this team, and what it misses.

Mixed. Gamma is a weak fit for earnings materials: externally hosted deck generation adds MNPI exposure and approved-template needs (judgment). Omitted: market-intelligence platforms such as AlphaSense, whose IR use cases cover script drafting, peer benchmarking and compliance/disclosure support [1], and which Bloomberg Tax describes as extracting details from filings and earnings transcripts for investors [2]; Workiva for disclosure workflows (Workiva named in [2] as a compliance platform).
