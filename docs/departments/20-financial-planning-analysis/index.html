AI use cases in practice

How teams like this actually use AI today, each with its source.

  • Drafting first-pass variance commentary from ledger detail with an LLM; the analyst checks every claim because drafts sound equally confident when wrong [1].
  • Natural-language questions on planning data inside an FP&A platform (for example 'why did Q2 revenue drop in EMEA?') returning data-backed explanations for monthly business reviews and board books [2].
  • Running multiple what-if scenarios (inflation, tariffs, supply shifts) on driver-based models in minutes instead of days, with analysts owning assumptions [1][2].
  • Drafting management reports by department or cost centre against budget from a trial balance, then having analysts add interpretation [3].
  • Baseline statistical or ML forecasts refreshed each cycle; one source cautions that simple statistical methods have beaten ML in published tests, so baselines are a starting point [1].

Capstone deliverable

The one real piece of work a learner produces to pass. It follows the handbook's assignment template.

A one-month variance pack for a sanitized or synthetic cost-centre dataset: AI-drafted commentary on the top 10 variances, each claim tied to a ledger line, plus a scenario table (base, downside, upside) with stated driver assumptions. Submit the documented prompts, the verification log showing corrections made to AI output, and measured time against a manual baseline. Sized for Advanced-tier hours.

Data risks and rules

The sensitive data this team handles, the law that applies, and the rules that follow.

Unreleased results and forecasts are material non-public information for listed companies (securities law, insider-trading and Reg FD exposure). Budgets carry compensation and headcount detail (employment law, GDPR if staff-identifiable). Rules: use only enterprise-licensed tools with no training on inputs, anonymise employee-level data, keep forecast files off consumer tools, keep a human reviewer on any number that leaves finance. Microsoft's Copilot in Excel FAQ says to avoid using Copilot for decisions in sensitive areas such as finance and to verify output [4]. AFP respondents cited data reliability (61%) as a barrier [1].

Adoption KPIs

Numbers to track so you know whether the training changed anything.

Share of FP&A staff using approved AI weekly (AFP benchmark: 23% use AI daily, weekly or monthly) [1]; hours to produce monthly variance commentary versus baseline; percentage of AI-drafted claims corrected at review; forecast error by horizon before and after; number of scenarios run per planning cycle.

Curriculum emphasis

Where the hours should go, and which tools to prioritise.

Spend most hours on hands-on variance commentary and scenario work with verification routines, then governance. Prioritise Excel Copilot (where the models live), Claude or ChatGPT Enterprise for narrative and long-document reasoning, and Power BI Copilot for natural-language data exploration. Teach reperformance of every number, because outputs read as authoritative even when wrong [1][4].

Tool-list fit

Whether the handbook's tool list suits this team, and what it misses.

The list fits well: Excel Copilot, Power BI Copilot and a general LLM cover the core jobs. Gemini and ChatGPT Enterprise overlap; one general assistant plus the Microsoft tools is enough for most teams (judgment). Omitted: dedicated planning platforms with embedded generative AI, such as OneStream SensibleAI, which offers narrative variance summaries and natural-language questions inside the planning model [2].

Jargon on this page

  • AI assistantA tool you talk to in plain language (Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot). It drafts, summarises and answers, but it can be wrong, so you check its work.
  • PromptThe instruction or question you give an AI tool. Clear prompts with context and a desired format get better results.
  • TierThe handbook's training depth: Core (6-10 hours per tool), Advanced (12-20), Specialist (20-40+). A department's tier sets how long to budget.
  • CopilotMicrosoft's name for its AI assistants inside Word, Excel, Teams, Power BI, security tools and Azure. One licence covers several of them.
  • GDPRThe EU's data protection law. Any AI use involving information about identifiable people has to respect it.
