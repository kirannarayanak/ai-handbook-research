AI use cases in practice

  • Drafting and reviewing safety statements, risk assessments and procedures with generative AI, with review by a competent person. [1]
  • Generating scenario-based safety training modules and quizzes tailored to a workforce. [1][2]
  • Summarising incident reports and spotting patterns; advisory only, not a replacement for formal risk analysis. [1]
  • Enhanced incident reporting with real-time risk detection, e.g. a virtual risk assistant tested in workplaces in Estonia, and predictive risk modelling in heavy manufacturing and insurance. [2]
  • Generating machine and equipment safety instructions from risk assessment and job hazard data, and recording incidents, near misses and observations on mobile (SAP EHS, vendor documentation). [3]
  • Computer vision on existing CCTV for site safety (behavioural safety, PPE detection) with agentic reporting for EHS teams (Protex, vendor material, not independently verified). [4]

Capstone deliverable

Advanced-tier capstone (about 8-12 hours): an AI-assisted safety pack for one task or site. Deliver a job hazard checklist and a toolbox-talk or training module with quiz, plus a summary of 10 anonymised incident narratives with themes. Include the prompt log, a competent-person sign-off sheet recording every correction to AI output, source checks against the applicable regulation, and time saved versus manual drafting.

Data risks and rules

Handles health and injury data, incident narratives naming workers, wellbeing and occupational health records, and CCTV footage. Regulatory hooks: GDPR (Ibec says no confidential or health-related data should go into AI tools unless anonymised and secured), the EU AI Act (transparency and mandatory human oversight) and, in Ireland, the Safety, Health and Welfare at Work Act 2005, under which legal responsibility stays with the human competent person. [1] Outside the EU, equivalents such as HIPAA and OSHA recordkeeping would apply (not covered by the sources). AI predictions are advisory only.

Adoption KPIs

Hours to produce a risk assessment or training module versus baseline; share of AI-drafted documents passing competent-person review without major correction; incident report completion time and near-miss reporting volume; number of trained EHS staff using approved tools; count of data-handling breaches or redaction failures (target zero).

Curriculum emphasis

Advanced tier: emphasise verification and governance over generation. Prioritise Claude or Gemini for drafting and incident summarisation (long-document handling), Microsoft 365 Copilot for documents inside the tenant, and Power BI Copilot for incident trend dashboards. Gamma only for training decks. Teach anonymisation, citing the regulation, and challenging AI summaries; the NSC review notes limited AI literacy and scepticism among EHS professionals and a need for clean, structured data. [2]

Tool-list fit

The list suits drafting, training material and trend analysis. Gamma is a weak fit for safety-critical work, as the sources show no use beyond presentation of content. The handbook omits EHS-platform assistants that the sources show are an embedded route: SAP EHS with Joule [3], and computer-vision platforms such as Protex [4]. These are vendor materials, so claims are unverified.
