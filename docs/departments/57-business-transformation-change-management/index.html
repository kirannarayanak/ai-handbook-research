AI use cases in practice

How teams like this actually use AI today, each with its source.

  • Guided change blueprints and change plans from a change-management-specific assistant (Prosci Kaiya); beta users reported two-hour sessions becoming ten-minute tasks (vendor claim) [1].
  • Drafting key change messages and tailoring communications to different audiences [1].
  • Developing resistance-management tactics for a given initiative [1].
  • Sentiment analysis, communication drafting and risk flagging as AI becomes part of everyday change work, per a vendor trends report [3].
  • Analysing change-adoption data against Gartner's finding that only 32% of leaders say their last change achieved healthy adoption, to target manager enablement and pacing [2][3].

Capstone deliverable

The one real piece of work a learner produces to pass. It follows the handbook's assignment template.

A change impact assessment and communications pack for one real or case-study initiative: AI-assisted impact matrix by stakeholder group, three audience-specific messages, and an adoption-tracking plan. Includes checked sources, documented prompts and time saved against a manual baseline.

Data risks and rules

The sensitive data this team handles, the law that applies, and the rules that follow.

Change work touches restructuring plans (employment law, consultation duties), employee survey and sentiment data (GDPR), and material non-public information on M&A or reorganisations (securities rules). Rules: no named-individual data in public models, aggregate and anonymise survey comments, keep unannounced restructuring out of non-enterprise tools, and have HR or Legal review any message that affects roles.

Adoption KPIs

Numbers to track so you know whether the training changed anything.

Time to produce a change plan or communication pack; share of initiatives with a documented impact assessment; adoption rate at 30/60/90 days; manager participation in change activities; employee sentiment trend.

Curriculum emphasis

Where the hours should go, and which tools to prioritise.

Prioritise Claude or ChatGPT Enterprise for impact analysis and audience-specific messaging, Microsoft 365 Copilot for working inside decks and mail, and Power BI Copilot for adoption analysis. Teach prompt design for communication tailoring and verification of any claimed statistics.

Tool-list fit

Whether the handbook's tool list suits this team, and what it misses.

The handbook list is broadly general-purpose and fits. It omits Prosci's Kaiya, a change-management-specific assistant drawing on Prosci research, available to enterprise clients [1]. Canva Magic Studio and Gamma are plausible for communications but are not evidenced in the opened sources. Note that the Gartner figure was seen only via a trade-press summary and Prosci material via its own press release.

Jargon on this page

  • PromptThe instruction or question you give an AI tool. Clear prompts with context and a desired format get better results.
  • Vendor claimA figure published by the company that sells the tool. It may be true, but no one independent has checked it.
  • CopilotMicrosoft's name for its AI assistants inside Word, Excel, Teams, Power BI, security tools and Azure. One licence covers several of them.
  • GDPRThe EU's data protection law. Any AI use involving information about identifiable people has to respect it.
