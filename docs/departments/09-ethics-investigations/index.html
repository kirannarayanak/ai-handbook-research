AI use cases in practice

How teams like this actually use AI today, each with its source.

  • Whistleblower response: interpreting complaints, generating follow-up questions and identifying relevant documents [1]
  • Early-stage triage: identifying and summarising key documents to decide whether a full investigation is needed (not individually verified) [1]
  • Time-sensitive matters: chronologies, patterns and hot documents to prioritise review (not individually verified) [1]
  • Detecting anomalous content such as code words or unusual calendar entries in conduct and fraud cases [1]
  • AI-powered translation inside case management so non-English reports can be investigated without waiting (vendor claim) [2]
  • Teams are cautiously adopting AI in intake, case management and reporting, per a 2024 benchmarking report [3]

Capstone deliverable

The one real piece of work a learner produces to pass. It follows the handbook's assignment template.

An investigation case file for a synthetic complaint: neutral case summary, evidence timeline, policy-comparison table and list of follow-up interview questions, all with source references. Include a note on what AI was and was not permitted to see (anonymisation step), prompts, human reviewer corrections and time saved. Advanced-tier hours.

Data risks and rules

The sensitive data this team handles, the law that applies, and the rules that follow.

Whistleblower identities, allegations about named individuals, health and employment data, privileged investigation material. Regulatory hooks: employment law, GDPR, whistleblower protection (the EU Whistleblower Directive and updated US Department of Justice guidance are noted in [2]) and legal privilege. Rules: minimise and pseudonymise data before AI use; AI must not make findings of fact or disciplinary recommendations; human oversight is stressed [1]; model type, jurisdiction and risk profile all affect whether AI is appropriate [1].

Adoption KPIs

Numbers to track so you know whether the training changed anything.

Days to complete case triage; time to build an evidence timeline; share of cases where AI use was documented and reviewed; reviewer correction rate; zero confidentiality breaches.

Curriculum emphasis

Where the hours should go, and which tools to prioritise.

Prioritise Claude and Microsoft 365 Copilot (summaries and timelines inside a secure tenant), ServiceNow Now Assist if cases live in ServiceNow, and a short module on ChatGPT Enterprise only where approved. Spend most time on confidentiality, anonymisation and human decision rules.

Tool-list fit

Whether the handbook's tool list suits this team, and what it misses.

General assistants fit drafting tasks but are a weaker fit for evidence handling than purpose-built investigation or case-management platforms. The handbook omits those (for example Case IQ and Mitratech, the vendors in [2][3]; vendor sources). ChatGPT Enterprise and Gemini need privilege and data-residency review before use on case files.

Jargon on this page

  • PromptThe instruction or question you give an AI tool. Clear prompts with context and a desired format get better results.
  • VerifiedVerified means a researcher opened the page and read the claim on it. Not verified means it came from a search snippet or a page that would not load, so treat it with care.
  • Vendor claimA figure published by the company that sells the tool. It may be true, but no one independent has checked it.
  • CopilotMicrosoft's name for its AI assistants inside Word, Excel, Teams, Power BI, security tools and Azure. One licence covers several of them.
  • GDPRThe EU's data protection law. Any AI use involving information about identifiable people has to respect it.
