Leadership, strategy and governance

All ten departments work mostly with long, unstructured, high-stakes documents (board papers, contracts, filings, case files, deal data rooms), so the shared AI pattern is summarise, compare, extract and draft, with a named human accountable for the output. The sources agree on the same controls: use approved enterprise tools, treat outputs as claims to verify (COSO's framing), keep an inventory of use cases, and scale human review to risk. Shared risks are privilege waiver and discoverability of AI transcripts, material non-public information and deal confidentiality, personal data in investigations (GDPR, whistleblower protections), and AI output that cannot be traced for audit or assurance. Evidence is thinnest for Office of the CEO, ERM and Ethics, where findings come from practitioner or vendor pieces rather than studies. Train together on a common module covering data classification, verification and prompt/output logging, with joint labs for Board Secretariat, Legal and Compliance (records and privilege), for Compliance, Internal Audit and ERM (controls evidence and COSO), and for Corporate Strategy and Corporate Development (scenario and deal analysis).

Fact-check notes · 11 corrections applied

I opened every cited URL (all returned OPENED) and checked the most surprising claims against the page text: EY adoption, Deloitte percentages, COSO six steps, Nestle and BCG figures, ACC survey, CSC survey, Ekimetrics figures, Optro 49%, Nasdaq Boardvantage, Harvey data-room claims. Nearly all checked claims held; corrections were small (wording, one unsupported use case, one unsupported sample size). Tool-capability claims (Datasite AI, CoCounsel, Acrobat AI, Now Assist) have no opened source and are flagged as not verified. Several sources are vendor or commentator pieces, so confidence is capped at medium for Compliance, Ethics and ESG.

HR, people and workplace

Across the nine departments the dominant pattern is gen AI as a first-draft and synthesis layer (job descriptions, policy answers, feedback and incident summaries, workforce narratives) with a human owning the final decision. The evidence base is mostly vendor and practitioner material: Workday reports a Hackett Group figure that 66% of HR teams use gen AI, but measured outcomes are scarce, so trainees should treat time-saved claims as hypotheses to measure locally. Shared risks are special-category and employee personal data (GDPR), employment-discrimination law, and the EU AI Act and NYC Local Law 144 rules for hiring and worker-management AI, which apply even when a person gives final sign-off. Human reviewers are named as necessary in the payroll and facilities sources; the safety-specific human-review claims (Ibec) could not be verified. Recommended joint training: one common module on employee-data handling, bias testing and human-in-the-loop decisions for departments 11-17 and 19; a second shared lab on HR-knowledge-base prompting for 11, 14, 16 and 18 (employee FAQs, ticket routing); and a safety/facilities pairing for 18 and 19 on incident and work-order summarisation.

Fact-check notes · 14 corrections applied

Fifteen of sixteen cited pages were opened and spot-checked against the specific figures and regulatory claims (Workday 66%, Gartner 95%, ATD 37%/70%/232, OpenSesame 8th to 5th, Deloitte 9%/74%/156, Project-B 94%/78%, Metaview dates, Praxikon Annex III and tool names, MiHCM HP $300M); nearly all matched. Corrections were small: Metaview's AI-disclosure duty falls on providers, Colorado enforcement is on hold, one MiHCM guidance claim was unsupported, and department 19 lost its Ibec claims because the page returned HTTP 403. Evidence remains largely vendor or practitioner material, and tool-capability claims for ServiceNow, Power Automate, LinkedIn Recruiter and Power BI Copilot were not checked against any opened page. Re-research of departments 18, 19 (second pass): All three Facilities Dive/JLL sources (dept 18) and all four dept 19 sources opened and supported the central claims, including the Lessen 20% and 1% figures, JLL Serve/Sclera, CBRE and Cushman and Wakefield items, the Ibec legal list (SHWW Act 2005, GDPR, EU AI Act 2024/1689) and NSC findings (Estonia, adoption barriers). Two unsupported product names (Corrigo, EHS Insight Copilot) were removed, and the Protex claim was narrowed to what the opened page shows. Vendor and trade-press material remains unverified for capability and performance claims, so confidence stays medium.

Finance, procurement and investor relations

All eight departments turn structured ledger, bank, spend or contract data into drafts, summaries and exception lists that a named human then reviews, so the shared skill is verification: reperforming numbers, tying output to source and documenting who signed off. Shared risks are confident-but-wrong output, material non-public information, personal and bank data, and weak evidence trails; sources describe COSO's February 2026 generative-AI control guidance and a Workiva finding that 1 in 4 executives said an AI error reached investors or the board. Microsoft itself warns against relying on Copilot in Excel for finance decisions. Training should be joint for a common core (data classification, review gates, prompt and evidence logging) and for three pairings: FP&A, Accounting and Treasury (variance, forecast and close narratives in spreadsheets); AP, Procurement and Vendor Management (P2P documents, contracts, supplier data and fraud signals); and FP&A with Investor Relations (earnings numbers and narrative consistency). Tax and IR need their own legal and disclosure modules.

Fact-check notes · 8 corrections applied

I opened and checked 17 cited pages, including every cited page except the CPA Practice Advisor article's later sections. Statistics and named capabilities matched their sources: COSO February 2026, Workiva 1 in 4, Gartner 35%, Flowserve, AFP 74% BEC and 2.9/5 AI policy score, Hackett 47%/9.9%, TR Institute percentages, SAP Joule/Concur Verify/Bid Analysis, and the Aeries pilot. Corrections were mostly wording precision, plus flags on three tool claims no opened page supports (CoCounsel, Now Assist, Perplexity/Gamma judgments). Overall reliability is good, but the Aeries pilot and Hackett/Simfoni figures are vendor-reported.

Sales, marketing and business development

All eight departments use AI for the same core jobs: research and synthesis, personalised first drafts, call and meeting summarisation, and reporting on CRM or campaign data. Sellers and marketers also report that data quality, not the model, is the limiting factor (Salesforce: 74% of AI-using sales teams prioritise data hygiene). Shared risks are personal data of prospects and contacts (UK/EU GDPR, PECR or e-privacy marketing consent), recorded-call consent (wiretap litigation against AI notetakers), confidential pipeline, pricing and customer data, and unverified AI claims reaching buyers. Vendor-reported gains are large but self-reported, and independent analyses of ad automation are more mixed, so training should teach measuring incremental results against a baseline. Train together on a common module covering CRM data hygiene, consent and recording rules, citation-checked research and a human review gate before external sending. Sales Ops with the two sales teams, Marketing with Digital Marketing, and Research with BD and Pre-Sales are natural pairings for hands-on labs.

I opened nearly all cited pages and most headline statistics (Salesforce 34%/36%/48%/55%/1.7x/74%/79% vs 54%/51%, Gong 130% and 1.8M opportunities, Verse.ai 25%, Cox 55%, Anthropic 30 minutes to 30 seconds, Qualtrics 71%/87%, Haus 640 tests, ICO PECR and controller/processor text) match their sources. One use case (Cyera registrations) and the SOX claim had no support and were removed or flagged; the ICO "absolute right to object to profiling" wording was corrected to what the page says. Several tool-fit claims (Einstein, NotebookLM, Canva, Power BI Copilot, RFP platforms) remain unverified and are labelled so. Vendor-authored statistics are self-reported, and departments 34 and 35 stay low confidence because their evidence is thin. Re-research of departments 34, 35 (second pass): All eight cited URLs opened and broadly support the claims attributed to them; the quantified presales figures (87%, 30 to 3-4 hours, 90%, 12-18 SME questions) match the AutoRFP.ai pages but are vendor-published. Department 34 evidence is mostly agency, vendor and analyst commentary describing expected capabilities, not measured results. One IDC-based claim was overreaching and was reworded; the Fiddler hallucination claim was softened. Tool-fit statements about Sales Navigator, Salesforce Einstein, Gamma, Crossbeam, PartnerStack, Loopio and Responsive remain unverified by the cited sources.

Customer and product

All five departments turn unstructured customer signals (tickets, calls, surveys, interview notes, feedback, usage data) into summaries, drafts and prioritised actions, and in each one the tool's output is a draft that a human must validate. The shared risks are customer personal data (GDPR/UK GDPR), unapproved "shadow AI" use, and confident but wrong output. Wrong output includes misread sentiment, invented user insight and mis-scored churn risk. Vendor-reported results (resolution rates, retention gains) are marketing claims and should not be used as training benchmarks. Customer Success, Support and CX should train together on voice-of-customer synthesis, data handling and human escalation rules. Product Management and Product Design & UX Research should train together on evidence-grounded PRDs, research synthesis and acceptance criteria. A joint cross-cluster session could use one shared feedback dataset, taking it from support tickets to themes to backlog items.

Fact-check notes · 9 corrections applied

All nine cited URLs opened and the main statistics (Zendesk 22%, 73%, 250%, 64%, Vagaro; Intercom 51%/86%/45 languages; Qualtrics 50 brands/90%; IdeaPlan 41%/73%; NN/g 841/92%) matched the pages. Errors found were mis-attributed or unsupported claims in Product Management and UX, now removed or recitified. Vendor and practitioner-blog figures remain marketing or indicative claims. Uncited handbook-gap statements (Dovetail, Forethought, Staircase AI) are labelled not verified.

Engineering, cloud and IT

All six departments use AI in the same way. An assistant drafts code, tests, infrastructure templates, runbooks or knowledge articles. A human then verifies the draft against something that can pass or fail: a test run, a plan or dry-run, or telemetry. Claude Code's own best-practice guidance says to give the agent a check it can run. Shared risks are source code and credentials pasted into prompts, hallucinated or insecure output, and agents acting with broad permissions on production systems or ERP. Departments 41-44 should train together on agentic coding, verification gates and secrets hygiene, and 43-45 together on cloud and ticket-system tooling. Department 46 should join 45 for ticket and knowledge workflows, and 46 should train with Finance on SAP data. Evidence quality is uneven. Most quantified claims come from vendors or from a single company's self-report, so managers should measure their own baselines. ServiceNow, Salesforce, Dynatrace and Cursor claims could not be verified because those pages could not be opened.

Fact-check notes · 22 corrections applied

All cited URLs opened, and most specific claims matched their sources (Zoominfo figures, Accenture RCT design, Meta 73%, AWS, Google, Azure, Datadog, Security Copilot, SAP). Errors found were misattributions and extrapolations rather than fabrications. Remaining weaknesses are heavy reliance on vendor pages and a single source for department 46, so the low confidence there and in 42 and 45 stands. Cursor, ServiceNow, Salesforce, Dynatrace and GitHub Copilot for pipeline files were not checked against any opened page. Re-research of departments 42, 45, 46 (second pass): All seven cited pages opened and supported the main statistics (WQR 19%, 67%, 60%, test-design lead; Gartner 69%; Joule 60% and 40% figures; the ITSM 72-hour definition and the 54% ServiceNow figure). The corrections were the removal of one unsupported claim, a few tightened wordings, and not-verified labels on tool names the sources do not cover. Evidence is mostly vendor-adjacent or opinion pieces (OpenText co-publishes the WQR, the ITSM author runs a competitor, ERP Today is promotional), so confidence stays medium, medium and low.

Security, data and AI

All five departments sit on sensitive data and on the platforms that hold it, so the strongest pattern is vendor-embedded AI that inherits existing access controls (Security Copilot, Purview, Snowflake RBAC, Power BI semantic models) and works best on well-prepared metadata. Shared risks are over-sharing through broad permissions, unverified AI output feeding decisions (alert verdicts, generated SQL, dashboard answers), and agents taking actions, so human approval gates and evaluation datasets are common requirements. Departments 47 and 48 should train together on SOC and governance handoffs and AI-agent oversight, and 49, 50 and 51 on semantic-layer preparation, answer validation and evaluation-driven development. All five should share one module on governing AI (NIST AI RMF and its generative AI profile) and on data classification. Research limit: most claims are vendor-reported, and regulatory hooks beyond NIST are general knowledge, not verified.

Fact-check notes · 20 corrections applied

All eleven distinct sources opened successfully. Core vendor claims (550%, 200 hours, ISO 42001, 98%/70% figures, CORTEX_USER granted to PUBLIC, Cortex no-training, NIST AI 600-1, Power BI geographic and SKU restrictions, Foundry governance, Purview AI app coverage) were confirmed on the cited pages. Several details were overstated or unsupported and were softened or removed, most notably the Genie Code rename, KQL, one-click policies and the Purview investigation scenario. Figures remain vendor-reported, and regulatory hooks other than NIST remain unverified, so confidence stays medium. Re-research of departments 52, 53 (second pass): All four cited pages opened and broadly support the use cases; WIPO figures, the Körber platform list and the Equal Experts guardrails and 'dozens of ADRs in a morning' match the sources. Corrections were mostly overstated or unsupported wording and unverified vendor claims. Only the two cited sources per department were opened, so tool-fit statements about other products remain unverified; confidence stays medium.

Operations, PMO and transformation

Research for this cluster is incomplete. Only two vendor pages (Atlassian Jira AI and UiPath Autopilot) were opened and both were re-opened and checked in verification. Other attempts failed (PMI returned 403; ServiceNow and McKinsey pages failed; the Microsoft and SAP pages held no usable product content). Every department therefore has at most one opened source, below the two-source target, and all are low confidence. Statements marked "not verified" come from general practice, not from an opened page. The expected shared pattern is that all seven departments turn semi-structured operational records (tickets, status updates, SOPs, audit findings, incident logs) into summaries, drafts and exception lists. The shared risks are confidential project, supplier and incident data, personal data in tickets and change feedback (GDPR), and unreviewed AI output entering formal records (SLAs, quality records, recovery plans). Suggested joint training is a shared module on process and ticket data hygiene and human review of AI outputs. Joint sessions are also suggested for 55/56/58 (process and SOP work), 54/57 (portfolio and change reporting), and 59 with 55 (incident communications). All of this needs verification before it goes into the handbook. A follow-up with fresh search budget is recommended.

Both opened sources (Atlassian Jira AI, UiPath Autopilot) exist and support the product capabilities attributed to them, including the 50% faster delivery vendor claim, the Work Readiness Checker, status drafting, BPMN capture and queue monitoring. A few phrasings overreached or were wrongly attributed and were corrected. The remaining content is mostly general-practice reasoning marked "not verified", and five departments have no source that supports their core use cases. Treat the cluster as a low-confidence draft; only Jira (54) and UiPath (56) tool claims are source-backed. Re-research of departments 55, 57, 58, 59, 60 (second pass): I opened all cited sources for all five departments and most claims were supported, including the Veeva Deviation Agent, Prosci Kaiya, the Gartner 32% figure, the DRJ/Forrester figures, Riskonnect, AlertMedia, Everbridge and SAP Joule.
