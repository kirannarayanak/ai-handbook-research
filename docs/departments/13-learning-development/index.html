AI use cases in practice

How teams like this actually use AI today, each with its source.

  • Outlining courses, storyboarding, writing learning objectives, generating narration and multimedia; 37% of surveyed designers said AI greatly cut course design time and 70% said it improved quality (232 respondents, sponsor-linked) [1]
  • AI role-play simulations for practising feedback, coaching and objection-handling conversations [2]
  • Qualitative analysis of open-ended feedback and survey comments, which rose from 8th to 5th in a 600+ professional L&D survey [2]
  • Personalised learning paths from skills data and role expectations [3]
  • Turning long reports and complex policies into short learner-friendly explainers and scenarios [3]

Capstone deliverable

The one real piece of work a learner produces to pass. It follows the handbook's assignment template.

A 30-minute micro-course on one real topic: learning objectives, 10-question quiz with answer rationale, a facilitator guide and a short visual deck, with every fact traced to a named source document, a prompt log and design hours versus the team's last comparable course.

Data risks and rules

The sensitive data this team handles, the law that applies, and the rules that follow.

Learner records and assessment results (GDPR), possible health or performance information in feedback comments, and third-party content under copyright. The ATD survey cites trust/accuracy and IP/copyright concerns as reasons designers distrust or avoid AI output [1]. Rules: ground content in approved source documents; fact-check every claim; do not paste confidential material into consumer tools; strip names from feedback before analysis.

Adoption KPIs

Numbers to track so you know whether the training changed anything.

Design hours per course hour vs baseline; share of courses with AI-assisted drafting; subject-matter-expert review cycles per course; learner satisfaction and quiz pass rate; factual errors found in review.

Curriculum emphasis

Where the hours should go, and which tools to prioritise.

Core tier: concentrate on grounded content generation and verification. Prioritise NotebookLM (source-grounded study material and quizzes), Claude or ChatGPT Enterprise for objectives and scenarios, and Gamma or Canva Magic Studio for delivery assets. Add a short block on analysing feedback data, since that is the fastest-growing use [2].

Tool-list fit

Whether the handbook's tool list suits this team, and what it misses.

Strong fit: the list covers drafting, grounded sources and visual output. Weak fit: none clearly. Not verified: dedicated learning-platform AI (LMS authoring and skills platforms) is omitted; Workday's page describes personalised paths inside the HCM, so Workday AI is arguably relevant here [3].

Jargon on this page

  • PromptThe instruction or question you give an AI tool. Clear prompts with context and a desired format get better results.
  • TierThe handbook's training depth: Core (6-10 hours per tool), Advanced (12-20), Specialist (20-40+). A department's tier sets how long to budget.
  • VerifiedVerified means a researcher opened the page and read the claim on it. Not verified means it came from a search snippet or a page that would not load, so treat it with care.
  • RAGRetrieval-augmented generation: the AI looks up your documents first and answers from them, which reduces but does not remove errors.
  • GDPRThe EU's data protection law. Any AI use involving information about identifiable people has to respect it.
