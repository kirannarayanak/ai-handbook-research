AI use cases in practice

How teams like this actually use AI today, each with its source.

  • Account and prospect research summarised from CRM, web and internal data; sellers expect agents to cut research time by 34% once fully implemented (survey of 4,050 sellers) [1]
  • Call summaries, next-step capture and multi-threading checks: Gong Labs found multi-threaded deals over $50K have 130% higher win rates, so AI flags single-threaded deals [2]
  • Meeting prep and at-risk deal queries in a copilot grounded in CRM, email and decks, e.g. 'a list of deals that are at risk of falling through' [4]
  • Professional-services firm pilot: early Copilot users reported about 3 hours saved per week, with CRM-based opportunity use cases [3]
  • Personalised outreach drafts reviewed by the rep; Salesforce reports agents contacted 130,000 leads and created 3,200 opportunities in four months [1]

Capstone deliverable

The one real piece of work a learner produces to pass. It follows the handbook's assignment template.

One strategic-account plan for a real or anonymised key account: AI-assisted account brief with every fact linked to a validated source, a stakeholder map flagging single-threaded risk, a call-summary-to-next-steps workflow, a prompt log, and a time-saved comparison (manual vs AI-assisted, minutes per task). Sized for Core-tier hours.

Data risks and rules

The sensitive data this team handles, the law that applies, and the rules that follow.

Customer confidential data, pricing, contract terms and pipeline are commercially sensitive; contact data is personal data under GDPR/UK GDPR. Call recordings need participant consent and notice: AI notetakers face wiretap-law class actions (federal and state wiretapping laws; e.g. Brewer v. Otter.ai, N.D. Cal. 2025) [5]. Rules: use only the enterprise-approved tool, announce recording, no pasting named customer data into consumer chatbots, human review before anything is sent.

Adoption KPIs

Numbers to track so you know whether the training changed anything.

Weekly active AI users per rep; minutes spent on pre-call research and CRM updates (before vs after); share of opportunities with 3+ buyer contacts; calls recorded with consent notice logged (%); follow-up email turnaround time.

Curriculum emphasis

Where the hours should go, and which tools to prioritise.

Spend most hours on account research with citation checks, call-summary workflows, and CRM-grounded prompting. Prioritise Salesforce Einstein and Gong AI because they work on the CRM and call data sellers already use; Microsoft 365 Copilot for email and meeting prep; Claude for synthesis. Teach consent and verification explicitly; Gartner-style reinvestment of saved time is a manager topic.

Tool-list fit

Whether the handbook's tool list suits this team, and what it misses.

Good fit overall: Einstein, Gong and Copilot match the cited use cases [1][2][4]. Gamma is a weak fit for core selling work (deck output, not research or CRM). Omission: AI meeting notetakers such as Otter and Fireflies are widely used and Otter is the subject of 2025 consent litigation, so the handbook should name the approved recorder and ban unapproved bots [5]. Sales agents in Microsoft 365 Copilot connect to Dynamics 365 and Salesforce, relevant if the firm is on either CRM [4]. Einstein and Gong capabilities beyond the cited pages: not verified.

Jargon on this page

  • AgentAn AI that does not just answer but takes actions: runs code, opens tickets, sends messages, edits systems. Agents need an approval step for anything that cannot be undone.
  • PromptThe instruction or question you give an AI tool. Clear prompts with context and a desired format get better results.
  • TierThe handbook's training depth: Core (6-10 hours per tool), Advanced (12-20), Specialist (20-40+). A department's tier sets how long to budget.
  • VerifiedVerified means a researcher opened the page and read the claim on it. Not verified means it came from a search snippet or a page that would not load, so treat it with care.
  • RAGRetrieval-augmented generation: the AI looks up your documents first and answers from them, which reduces but does not remove errors.
  • CopilotMicrosoft's name for its AI assistants inside Word, Excel, Teams, Power BI, security tools and Azure. One licence covers several of them.
  • GDPRThe EU's data protection law. Any AI use involving information about identifiable people has to respect it.
