AI use cases in practice

How teams like this actually use AI today, each with its source.

  • Discover and monitor generative AI use via Data Security Posture Management (DSPM) for AI as the front door to discover, secure and apply compliance controls for AI usage (one-click policies not verified) [1]
  • Use sensitivity labels so AI apps only return data a user may access [1]
  • Assess AI apps in use across Microsoft Copilots (incl. Security Copilot, Copilot in Fabric), Entra-registered apps, ChatGPT Enterprise and Claude Enterprise, and other AI apps [1]
  • Structure AI risk assessments around the NIST AI RMF and its Generative AI Profile (NIST-AI-600-1, July 2024) [2]
  • Security Copilot agents span Defender, Entra, Intune and Purview to help detect, investigate and respond (specific Purview data-security-investigation scenario not verified) [3]
  • Draft policy-to-control mappings and privacy workflow text for human review (Claude, Gemini) - general practice, not verified in sources

Capstone deliverable

The one real piece of work a learner produces to pass. It follows the handbook's assignment template.

A one-page AI risk assessment for one approved AI tool, mapped to NIST AI RMF functions and the Generative AI Profile, listing data categories, label and permission checks, residual risks and owner. Include documented prompts, the sources used, and time saved against a manual assessment.

Data risks and rules

The sensitive data this team handles, the law that applies, and the rules that follow.

Holds policies, audit evidence, privacy requests and risk registers, which can contain personal data and security weaknesses. Hooks: GDPR and other privacy law, sector rules (not verified in sources). Rules: classify before AI access; fix over-permissioned content first, since generative AI amplifies oversharing [1]; keep evidence of AI decisions; never paste vulnerability details into unapproved tools.

Adoption KPIs

Numbers to track so you know whether the training changed anything.

Share of AI tools with a completed risk assessment; percentage of sensitive content labelled; DSPM-for-AI risk findings closed; time to complete an assessment; policy exceptions raised for shadow AI.

Curriculum emphasis

Where the hours should go, and which tools to prioritise.

Prioritise Microsoft Purview (labels, DSPM for AI) and Microsoft Security Copilot because they enforce controls, then ServiceNow Now Assist for workflow evidence and Claude for drafting and mapping with human review. Time goes to framework literacy (NIST) and validating outputs.

Tool-list fit

Whether the handbook's tool list suits this team, and what it misses.

Purview fits strongly and is documented as covering many AI apps [1]. ServiceNow Now Assist was not verified in an opened source. Gemini and Claude are drafting aids only. The handbook omits a framework resource such as NIST AI RMF, which is not a tool but belongs in training [2].

Jargon on this page

  • AgentAn AI that does not just answer but takes actions: runs code, opens tickets, sends messages, edits systems. Agents need an approval step for anything that cannot be undone.
  • PromptThe instruction or question you give an AI tool. Clear prompts with context and a desired format get better results.
  • Shadow AIStaff using AI tools the company has not approved, often personal accounts, which can leak confidential data.
  • VerifiedVerified means a researcher opened the page and read the claim on it. Not verified means it came from a search snippet or a page that would not load, so treat it with care.
  • CopilotMicrosoft's name for its AI assistants inside Word, Excel, Teams, Power BI, security tools and Azure. One licence covers several of them.
  • GDPRThe EU's data protection law. Any AI use involving information about identifiable people has to respect it.
