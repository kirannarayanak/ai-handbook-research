Coverage matrix

Who uses what, at a glance

Start high: eight clusters against ten tool families, coloured by how many department-tool recommendations fall in each cell. Then drill into the full 60-by-57 grid, filter by tier or family, and click any cell to open the department or the tool.

First time here? How to read the matrix (click to hide; stays hidden on this browser)
  1. 1
    Rows are teams, columns are tool families

    The high-level view groups 60 departments into eight clusters and 57 tools into ten families. A number is how many handbook recommendations land in that cell; darker means more.

  2. 2
    Switch to the detailed grid

    Click 'Detailed grid' to see every department against every tool. A filled dot means the handbook recommends that tool for that department.

  3. 3
    Filter, then click

    Narrow by tier, family or cluster. Tick 'Hide the six general assistants' to see the specialist pattern. Click any cell or name to open the department or tool.

How to read it

A filled cell means the handbook recommends that tool for that department. Darker cells in the high-level view mean more recommendations between a cluster and a tool family. The six general assistants dominate every row; hide them to see the specialist pattern underneath.

What it is for

Rationalising licences (which families cover which clusters), planning shared modules (clusters that share families can train together), and spotting gaps (clusters with no specialist platform at all). Confidence and hours live on the detail pages.
